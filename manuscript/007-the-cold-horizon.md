# Chapter 7: The Cold Horizon

The air reeked of damp earth and something sour, a smell that clung to the skin and settled in the lungs. Gabriel adjusted his oxygen mask as he surveyed the skeletal remains of what used to be the town square. The once-bustling plaza was now choked with vines, pulsating faintly with the sickly glow of the alien spores. He heard the faint crackle of his radio.

“Bravo team, report. Any movement?”

Gabriel pressed the button on his chest rig. “All quiet for now, Captain. Citizens are almost loaded into transport. ETA five minutes.”

“Good. Keep it that way.”

He stepped back, scanning the perimeter for any signs of the life forms they’d come to fear. Twisted amalgamations of flesh and plant-like growths, they had been humans once—or animals, or something else entirely. Now, they were unrecognizable, their bodies warped by the meteor’s mutagenic spores.

“Gabe!” a voice called. Jenna, his partner on the squad, waved him over. “We’ve got another group lagging behind. Block A, building four.”

“Great,” he muttered under his breath. “Just what we needed.”

He jogged toward the building, his boots crunching against the frosted ground. Temperatures had dipped as they moved north, but it wasn’t enough to stop the spread. Only the frozen wastelands were truly safe, and even those were filling fast with refugees.

The building was an old, abandoned structure, its walls covered in layers of grime and forgotten memories. Broken windows let in slivers of light, casting long shadows across the cracked floor. Dust swirled in the still air, and the faint sound of dripping water echoed through the empty halls. Despite the decay, there was a quiet, eerie stillness, as though the building itself had long been forgotten by time.

Gabriel pushed the door open, the hinges groaning in protest. Inside, the air was musty and still. He swept his flashlight across the room and froze.

A cryopod stood in the center, its glass frosted over but intact. Unlike the other decayed tech scattered throughout the ruins, this pod looked pristine, almost untouched by time or the elements.

“Jenna, you seeing this?” he called over the radio.

“Hold on—what are you talking about?”

“There’s… a pod here. Looks operational.”

He approached cautiously, wiping the glass with his glove. His breath caught in his throat. Inside was a woman, pale and serene, her dark hair floating around her head like ink in water with subtle strands of silver threaded through it, catching the light like whispers of moonlight. Her eyes were closed, but her expression was troubled, as if she were locked in a dream she couldn’t escape.

“Gabe, what’s the hold-up?” Jenna’s voice crackled.

“We’ve got a survivor,” he muttered, his tone edged with urgency. “I think she’s been in cold sleep… for a long time.” His gaze lingered on the frost-lined glass of the pod, the faint rise and fall of the woman’s chest confirming she was still alive—barely.”

He could hear Jenna cursing faintly on the other end. “Can you move her?”

“Pod looks portable, but I’ll need backup. This thing’s heavy.”

Before Jenna could respond, a low, guttural sound echoed through the building. Gabriel’s stomach dropped.

The life forms had found them.
