# Chapter 31: Harrow’s Betrayal

Harrow waited until the outpost trusted exhaustion more than locks.

The transport crew slept around the command room in borrowed blankets. One guard watched the perimeter screens; another had gone to check the generator. Gabriel sat against the wall with his boots still on, asleep before he had finished cleaning his rifle.

Ava held the outer watch. Harrow could see her through a slit in the barricade, a narrow figure beneath the floodlights, attention fixed on the dark.

He crossed to the storage room.

The lock surrendered to a strip of spring steel and patience. Inside, the recovered hospital records filled two sealed cases. He had imagined carrying both. The cases were heavier than they looked, and the notebook already pulled at one side of his coat.

He opened the first case and took what would fit in his pack: assay tables, handwritten formulas, the slim folders with intact seals. The bulkier binders stayed behind.

Enough to sell, he told himself. Enough to buy filters, medicine, a place behind somebody else’s walls.

He left the door almost closed.

***

Outside, Ava turned at the small wrong sound of its latch.

She entered the command room and touched Gabriel’s boot. He woke with his hand closing on the rifle.

“Storage,” she said.

They found the picked lock, the open case, and Harrow’s empty bedroll. Gabriel pulled his mask from its pouch and sealed it before they went beyond the perimeter.

Gabriel did not waste time swearing. “North maintenance corridor. He asked about it when we arrived.”

They caught sight of Harrow at the far barricade. He had disabled one section of the perimeter alarm and squeezed through a drainage gap beyond it.

“Harrow!” Gabriel called.

The scavenger ran.

The drain widened into an old utility tunnel. Harrow turned at the first junction without slowing. Ava could have run ahead of Gabriel, but she could not see what waited around the bend. She kept close enough to hear his breathing.

They cornered Harrow in a collapsed pump chamber. Fallen pipework blocked a drainage opening behind him. He turned with a compact demolition charge in one hand and his pack over his shoulder.

“Drop it,” Gabriel said.

“You think those notes belong to you?” Harrow’s face shone with sweat. “Your carrier will lock them in a laboratory. I can turn them into food. Power. Protection.”

“For whom?” Ava asked.

“For people who can use them.”

“Name one.”

His grip tightened around the charge.

Gabriel kept the rifle trained low, away from the pack. “Put the trigger down. We walk back together.”

“Back to a cell.”

“Back alive.”

For one moment, Harrow looked tired enough to agree. Then footsteps sounded in the tunnel behind Ava and Gabriel. The outpost guard called for them to wait. Harrow jerked toward the voice, his hand closing around the trigger.

He threw the charge at the ceiling.

Ava hit Gabriel before the blast did.

Concrete and rusted pipe came down in a single grey wave. Something tore across her back. A jagged slab struck Gabriel’s leg; she heard him cry out before his head hit the edge of a valve housing.

When Ava could hear again, the tunnel had changed shape.

Rubble sealed half the chamber. Harrow’s lamp jerked away through a gap on the far side, then vanished. Gabriel lay motionless under her, blood running from his temple into the dust.

The mutation answered her terror. Amber light threaded both arms. Broken tissue in her back pulled together around grit and cloth while pressure built beneath her shoulder blades, demanding more room.

She could tear through the blockage. She could follow Harrow. The thought arrived whole and bright: catch him, open him, take back what was theirs.

Gabriel made a wet sound when he breathed.

Ava chose him.

She rolled the fallen pipe from his leg and pressed two fingers to his throat. A pulse. Too fast, but present. The outpost guard shouted from the other side of the debris.

“Medical kit,” Ava called. Her voice did not sound human enough. She tried again. “Bring the kit. Now.”

She knelt over Gabriel and counted his breaths until the spikes beneath her skin withdrew.

By the time the crew opened a path into the chamber, he had begun to stir. His eyes opened without focusing.

“Don’t move,” Ava said.

“Wasn’t planning to.” His words slurred at the edges. “Harrow?”

“Gone.”

“The cases,” Ava said to the guard. “In storage. Lock the room. He left the binders.”

The guard relayed the message while another crew member braced Gabriel’s injured leg. Ava held the dressing against his temple.

“He didn’t take everything,” she told him.

Gabriel reached for her wrist. His grip was weak but deliberate. “Then we protect what’s left.”

No vow of revenge followed. There were wounds to bind, a blocked tunnel to shore up, and frightened people above them who needed to know whether the outpost was secure.

When they carried Gabriel back into the outpost, the storage guard opened the remaining cases for Ava to check. Gaps showed where Harrow had pulled the thin folders free. The binders were still there. She shut the lids and waited until she heard the lock turn.
