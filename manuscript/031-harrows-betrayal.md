# Chapter 31: Harrow’s Betrayal

The outpost was quiet under the dim glow of makeshift floodlights. The survivors and transport team rested in scattered corners, the tension of the day giving way to exhaustion. Gabriel sat near the command table, cleaning his rifle in silence, while Ava lingered by the perimeter, her eyes scanning the horizon. Harrow sat alone, clutching the notebook, his thoughts racing.

“This could change everything,” Harrow whispered to himself, his fingers tracing the edges of the notes. The thought of their worth, the power they held, was intoxicating. He glanced toward Gabriel and Ava, their backs turned. This was his chance.

The opportunity came during the early hours of the morning. The transport team had left the storage room lightly guarded, trusting the security of the outpost. Harrow slipped through the corridors, his footsteps soft against the concrete. He reached the storage room, his pulse pounding.

Inside, he found the secured case containing rest of the notes. The lock wasn’t as complex as he’d feared, and after a few tense moments, it clicked open. Harrow worked quickly, pulling out the contents. The stack was heavier than he expected, and he realized he couldn’t carry it all without being noticed. Frustrated but determined, he stuffed as much as he could into his bag, leaving behind the bulkier files.

Ava felt it first—a shift in the air, a quiet unease. She turned from her watch post, her eyes narrowing as she studied the camp, her gaze darting to every corner. Her gaze landed on the storage room door, which was slightly ajar. Her stomach twisted.

“Gabriel,” she called softly, her voice taut.

Gabriel was on his feet in an instant, his rifle in hand. He followed Ava’s line of sight, his jaw tightening. Together, they moved toward the storage room, their steps quick but quiet. Inside, the sight of the open case confirmed their worst fears.

“He’s gone,” Ava said, her voice cold.

Gabriel’s gaze darkened. “He won’t get far.”

Harrow moved swiftly through the darkened corridors of the outpost, his heart pounding. He had planned his escape meticulously, disabling a small section of the perimeter defenses earlier that day. The gap he’d created led to a narrow tunnel just beyond the barricades. If he could reach it, he’d be free.

As he approached the gap, a faint voice echoed behind him. “Harrow!”

He froze, glancing back to see Ava and Gabriel closing in. Panic flared in his chest. He turned and ran, his bag bouncing against his side.

The chase was frantic. Harrow ducked through the tunnel, his breath ragged as he navigated the narrow, winding path. Gabriel and Ava followed, their footsteps echoing in the confined space. The tunnel opened into a crumbling underground chamber, its walls lined with old pipes and debris.

“Harrow, stop!” Gabriel shouted, his voice reverberating off the walls.

Harrow skidded to a halt, turning to face them. His eyes were wild, his chest heaving. “You don’t understand! These notes—they’re worth more than you realize. We could rebuild everything! Civilization! We don’t have to keep living like this!”

Ava stood firm, her voice cutting through the tension. "At what cost, Harrow? Betraying the people who trusted you?"

Harrow’s grip on his bag tightened. “You don’t see the bigger picture! With these notes, we can buy resources, protection—anything we need. I’m doing this for all of us!”

Gabriel’s rifle didn’t waver. “That’s not your call to make. Drop the bag.”

Harrow hesitated, his gaze flicking between them. Then, in a desperate move, he grabbed a small explosive device from his belt. “I’ll do it!” he shouted. “I’ll blow the rest of the notes if you come any closer!”

Ava’s eyes widened, panic gripping her chest. Gabriel took a step forward, his rifle still raised, but Ava quickly reached out, her arm trembling as she blocked him. "No! Stay back," she stammered, her voice strained and pleading. "He’ll do it!"

Harrow’s hand was already on the trigger. He threw the device toward the tunnel entrance. Ava reacted without hesitation, throwing herself over Gabriel just as the explosion ripped through the air. The blast sent debris flying, and a jagged stone struck Gabriel’s leg, drawing a sharp cry of pain before he collapsed. Another piece of rubble struck his head, knocking him unconscious. Ava took the brunt of the explosion, her back shredded by flying shrapnel.

She gasped as pain flared through her body, her amber veins glowing intensely as her mutation began to heal the wounds. Her breathing was shallow and uneven, and she clenched her fists, fighting the overwhelming urge to lose control. Her vision blurred, but one thought anchored her: Gabriel.

Ignoring her own pain, she crawled to his side, her trembling hands gently cradling his head. "Gabriel! Wake up!" she pleaded, her voice cracking. Her body shook, her mutation battling to heal her injuries as she fought against the primal surge threatening to take over.

Ava’s amber eyes glowed faintly as she steadied her breathing, her focus entirely on Gabriel. She whispered softly, "Stay with me, Gabriel. I need you."

The sound of Harrow’s footsteps fading into the distance didn’t even register. Ava tightened her grip on Gabriel, choosing to protect him over pursuing the traitor.

“Ava!” Gabriel shouted, steadying her as the dust began to settle. Her amber veins glowed faintly as her body worked to heal the wound, but the strain was evident on her face. She clenched her fists, her breathing ragged as the edges of control frayed.

“I’m fine,” she said through gritted teeth, though her trembling hands betrayed her. Her eyes flicked to the blocked tunnel and then to Gabriel. “Harrow’s gone.”

Gabriel’s gaze softened as he held her steady, his voice low and filled with emotion. "Ava, you saved me," he said, his words trembling with both gratitude and pain. He brushed a hand over her cheek, his thumb gently wiping away a tear. "I’m glad you’re you, Ava. Otherwise, I might’ve lost you.""

When the dust settled, Ava knelt beside Gabriel, her body trembling from exhaustion and pain. The tunnel entrance was blocked, and Harrow was gone. Slowly, Gabriel stirred, groaning as he tried to sit up. Ava pressed a hand gently against his chest to stop him.

"Don’t move," she said softly, her voice thick with relief. Her eyes flicked to the shattered case nearby. She crawled toward it, her body protesting every movement. Her hands shook as she examined the remaining notes.

“He didn’t take everything,” she said quietly. “We still have enough.”

Gabriel rested a hand on her shoulder, his voice steady. “He’ll pay for this. One way or another.”

Ava nodded, her jaw tightening. The betrayal stung, but the mission wasn’t over. They still had the notes—and with them, a chance to uncover the truth.

Far away, Harrow emerged from the ruins, his bag clutched tightly. His heart raced, the adrenaline still coursing through his veins. He glanced back once, then vanished into the night, his mind already calculating his next move.
