# Chapter 12: Shadows of the Past

Ava stood near the edge of the landing platform, her eyes drifting over the endless expanse of white snow and jagged ice stretching out below. The ship hovered gently above the frozen landscape, its engines humming softly against the quiet vastness. The cold air that seeped through the open hatch carried a crisp, untouched purity, a stark contrast to the sterile interiors she had grown accustomed to.

For the first time since she woke up, she felt a strange sense of freedom—though it was limited, confined to the staff-only areas. Yet, even this small taste of the outside world was enough to unsettle something deep within her. The untouched, frozen wasteland stretched beyond sight, beautiful yet desolate, reminding her that the world she once knew was long gone.

She wrapped her arms around herself, not from the cold, but from the unfamiliar weight of it all. The memories that eluded her, the future that remained uncertain—it all felt as vast and uncharted as the frozen horizon before her.

Gabriel leaned against a railing a short distance away, watching her. He hadn’t spoken much since Jenna had announced Ava’s clearance to move around, though he’d been by her side the entire time.

It wasn’t that he didn’t have anything to say—it was that he wasn’t sure how to say it. Seeing her out here, standing on the platform with the vast, frozen world stretching before her, made something tighten in his chest. She looked small against the endless white, yet there was a quiet strength in the way she held herself, as if she were searching for something in the horizon that even she didn’t understand.

She hadn’t asked him why he lingered nearby, and he hadn’t offered an explanation. Maybe she assumed it was protocol, or maybe she simply didn’t care. But the truth was, he didn’t like the idea of her being alone with her thoughts for too long. Not after everything she’d been through.

Gabriel exhaled slowly, shifting his grip on the railing. He wasn’t used to feeling uncertain—not about his mission, not about the people around him. But Ava wasn’t like anyone he’d ever met, and the more time he spent around her, the more he found himself questioning things he once thought were simple.

She turned to him, the wind tugging at her silver-threaded hair. “It’s… quiet out here,” she said softly.

“It is,” Gabriel replied, stepping closer. “You don’t get much peace like this anymore. Not with what’s out there.”

Ava nodded, her gaze drifting back to the horizon. But her mind wasn’t on the present—it was on fragments of something else, something buried deep in her memory.

Flashes of sterile white rooms. Needles piercing her skin. The hum of machines and the cold, unfeeling voices of scientists.

Her chest tightened, and she wrapped her arms around herself, trying to shake off the images.

“You okay?” Gabriel’s words cut through her thoughts, gentle but laced with concern.

Ava blinked, realizing she had started trembling. “I… I think I’m remembering things,” she said hesitantly.

Gabriel’s brow furrowed, and he stepped closer, his presence grounding. “What kind of things?”

Ava hesitated. “Bad things,” she admitted, her voice barely above a whisper.

The memories came in waves, disjointed but vivid. She saw herself in a greenhouse, tending to rows of vibrant plants. She remembered the warmth of the sun on her skin, the smell of damp soil, and the joy of nurturing life.

Then came the sickness. It started as an unshakable fatigue, a weight pressing down on her limbs, making even the simplest tasks in the greenhouse exhausting. At first, Ava dismissed it—blaming long hours, the humid air, and the stress of her research. But the exhaustion grew worse, followed by fever, relentless and burning, leaving her body weak and trembling.

She remembered the hospital—the sterile white walls, the quiet murmurs of doctors who never met her gaze. They spoke in hushed tones, running tests, each result deepening their frowns. And then the verdict came: an unidentified illness. Terminal.

She should have been afraid, but instead, she had felt numb. The diagnosis unraveled everything she had built—her work, her future. She had been a botanist, a scientist, someone who nurtured life. Now, she was just another patient waiting for the inevitable.

Then, one night, everything changed.

Heavy footsteps outside her hospital room jolted her awake. Groggy and disoriented, she barely had time to react before masked figures entered. They moved with chilling precision, their dark uniforms blending into the shadows. They didn’t speak. They didn’t hesitate.

A needle pierced her arm before she could resist, cold liquid rushing into her veins. Darkness swallowed her before she could scream.

When she awoke, the beeping of hospital monitors was gone. The crisp sheets, the faint scent of antiseptic—gone. Instead, she found herself in a sterile, steel-walled room, the air thick with the hum of machines. Her arms and legs were restrained against a medical table, cold metal biting into her skin.

She wasn’t in a hospital anymore.

She was in a lab.

The men in suits had taken her, but they hadn’t come to save her. She was no longer a patient. She was a specimen.

They studied her, experimented on her. Needles, scans, and injections burned through her veins, each test more invasive than the last. They spoke of the meteorite, of mutations, of a “potential breakthrough.” But not once did they speak to her, not once did they see her as anything more than data on a screen.

And then, one day, everything escalated.

Her restraints grew tighter. The injections became more painful. The scientists spoke of “introducing the spores.”

That was the first time she truly felt afraid.

Ava clenched her fists, her nails digging into her palms.

Gabriel noticed her distress and moved closer, his voice gentle. “You don’t have to talk about it if you’re not ready.”

“I don’t want to remember,” Ava said, her voice cracking.

Gabriel placed a hand on her shoulder, careful not to startle her. “It’s okay to feel that way. Whatever happened to you, it wasn’t fair. You didn’t deserve it.”

Ava’s amber eyes met his, glistening with unshed tears. “They… treated me like I wasn’t human. Like I didn’t matter.”

Gabriel’s jaw tightened, and he felt a surge of anger on her behalf. “You do matter,” he said firmly. “More than they ever realized.”

Ava looked away, the wind tugging at her hair. “I don’t even know who I am anymore. I was a botanist before all of this. I loved plants… creating life. Now, I feel like everything I touch just… destroys.”

Gabriel stepped in front of her, forcing her to meet his gaze. “You saved us, Ava. You’re not a destroyer. You’re a survivor. And that’s not something to be ashamed of.”

She didn’t respond, but the tension in her shoulders eased slightly.

As they made their way back inside the ship, Gabriel’s mind churned. He couldn’t shake the weight of her words or the anger he felt toward whoever had put her through such pain.

One day. They were one day away from the checkpoint.

The plan was to hand Ava over to the authorities for further study, but now Gabriel wasn’t sure if he could go through with it. The people at the checkpoint might see her as a threat, as something to be contained and experimented on, just like before.

She deserved better than that.

“You’re quiet,” Ava said, breaking the silence as they walked through the ship’s narrow corridors.

Gabriel glanced at her, his expression conflicted. “Just thinking.”

“About what?”

He hesitated, then decided to be honest. “About you. And what’s going to happen when we reach the checkpoint.”

Ava’s steps faltered, and she looked down at the floor. “They’re going to lock me up again, aren’t they?”

Gabriel didn’t answer right away. “I don’t know,” he admitted. “But I won’t let them hurt you.”

She looked at him, her amber eyes searching his. “Why do you care so much?”

“Because you deserve someone who does,” he said simply.

For a moment, Ava didn’t say anything. Then, slowly, she nodded.

That night, as Gabriel sat in his bunk, he found himself unable to sleep. His thoughts kept returning to Ava—her pain, her resilience, and the way she had begun to trust him despite everything she’d been through.

He didn’t know what the right answer was. All he knew was that Ava wasn’t just another survivor. She was something special.

And he wasn’t going to let her down.
