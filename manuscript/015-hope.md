# Chapter 15: Hope

Barefoot, Ava stood in the corridor, the cool metal floor grounding her amidst the ship's gentle hum. The faint hum of the ship’s systems vibrated beneath her, a steady rhythm that felt oddly comforting. She trailed her fingers along the wall, the surface smooth and unfamiliar. Everything here was alien to her, yet strangely safe – a paradox she couldn’t quite resolve.

Her gaze flicked to the viewport at the far end of the corridor. Beyond it, stars glimmered like shards of broken glass scattered across the darkness. She’d seen them before, hadn’t she? Somewhere in the fragments of her mind, the memory lingered, just out of reach. She took a step closer, drawn by the void’s quiet allure.

“It’s mesmerizing, isn’t it?”

The voice startled her. Ava turned sharply to find Gabriel leaning casually against the wall, his arms crossed, a faint smile on his lips. His uniform, worn but familiar, paired with a quiet kindness in his eyes, softened his otherwise stern features.

“Sorry,” he said, raising his hands in mock surrender. “Didn’t mean to sneak up on you.”

Ava shook her head, looking back at the viewport. “It’s fine. I was just… thinking.”

“About?” Gabriel stepped closer, his boots making a soft thud against the floor.

A brief silence fell as she considered her response. How could she explain the jumble of thoughts and feelings tangled in her mind? “I don’t know,” she admitted. “Everything. Nothing.”

He nodded as if he understood. “That’s fair. It’s a lot to take in.”

They stood in silence for a moment, watching the stars. Ava glanced at him from the corner of her eye. He seemed so at ease here, so certain of his place in the world. She envied that.

“Come on,” Gabriel said suddenly, breaking the silence. “Let me show you around. It’ll help you get your bearings.”

Ava hesitated, but the warmth in his tone was impossible to resist. She nodded, and he gestured for her to follow.

The tour was informal, more like a conversation than an introduction to the ship. Gabriel led her through the main areas, pointing out the essentials: the mess hall, the sleeping quarters, the engineering bay. He explained the purpose of each space with a mixture of practicality and humor that made Ava feel at ease despite the curious stares from passing crew members.

“The engine room’s off-limits,” he said with a wink. “Not because of any real danger, but because if the chief engineer catches you in there, you’ll never hear the end of it.”

Ava managed a small smile, the first in what felt like forever. Gabriel seemed to notice, and his grin widened.

They paused in the mess hall, where a handful of crew members were seated at tables, their conversations hushed. The chatter faded as Ava stepped into the room. She felt their eyes on her, the weight of their mistrust pressing down like a physical force. Her fingers curled into fists at her sides.

Gabriel placed a reassuring hand on her shoulder. “It’ll take time,” he said softly. “They just don’t know you yet.”

Ava nodded, though she wasn’t sure she believed him. Time hadn’t been kind to her so far.

By the time they reached the observation deck, Ava’s head was spinning with new information. Gabriel leaned against the railing, arms crossed as he looked out at the stars.

“What do you think?” he asked.

Ava paused, searching for the right words. “It’s… overwhelming,” she admitted. “But it’s better than I expected.”

He chuckled. “High praise coming from you.”

She glanced at him sharply, but his grin was disarming. For the first time since waking up in this strange new world, she felt a flicker of something she couldn’t quite name. Perhaps not hope, but something that resembled it.

“You’ll find your place here,” Gabriel said, his tone serious now. “It won’t happen overnight, but it will happen. Trust me on that.”

Ava looked back at the stars, her reflection ghosted faintly on the glass. “I want to believe that,” she said quietly.

“You will.”

They stood there for a while, side by side, as the stars drifted by in silence.

The mess hall was quieter than usual. Ava sat at the farthest table, her hands wrapped around a steaming cup of something Gabriel had called "caf." The drink was bitter, almost medicinal, but its warmth seeped into her fingers, grounding her. The room wasn’t empty, but the crew members present kept their distance, their conversations hushed and glances quick.

Ava wasn’t surprised. The way they looked at her—a mix of curiosity and wariness—was something she was growing used to. It was better than outright hostility, she supposed. Still, she couldn’t ignore the whispers she’d overheard earlier:

“She’s the one from the fortress, isn’t she?”

“Yeah. Remember how cold-resistant mutations hit that place hard? People were already on edge, and then she… lost control. Took out most of the guards in the confusion.”

“Why is she even here? Didn’t she kill half the squad trying to stop her? She is a freak...”

“Not half,” another voice interjected, quieter but firm. “And Gabriel found her long before that. She’s not the reason the fortress fell—she’s just… complicated.”

“Complicated and dangerous,” the first voice muttered. “I don’t care what Gabriel says, she shouldn’t be here.”

The words stung, even though she tried to brush them off. Her eyes prickled with unshed tears, and she quickly lowered her gaze to the steaming cup in her hands. She took a shaky breath, trying to steady herself, but the knot in her chest refused to loosen.

Gabriel entered the room, his presence immediately drawing attention. He carried an air of calm authority that seemed to put people at ease. Spotting Ava, he made his way over, a tray balanced in one hand. As he placed it on the table and slid into the seat across from her, his eyes lingered on her face.

"You okay?" he asked gently, his tone probing but not pressing.

Ava forced a smile and shook her head. "It’s nothing," she murmured, her voice wavering just enough to betray her. Gabriel didn’t press further, but his expression softened, a quiet understanding passing between them.

Gabriel nodded. “You’ll find your footing. Everyone does eventually.”

She wasn’t sure she believed him, but his words were oddly comforting. She sipped her drink, the bitterness less jarring now.

The mess hall was quiet apart from the occasional clink of dishes as Gabriel finished his meal. He glanced at her, studying her for a moment, then leaned back in his chair, a subtle grin playing on his lips. Ava shifted in her seat, her hands tightening around her cup as the silence stretched, not entirely uncomfortable but heavy with unspoken things. Gabriel exhaled, pushing his empty plate aside and sitting forward.

“Come on,” he said, standing. “I want you to meet some people.”

Ava stiffened. “Do I have to?”

Gabriel’s smile was patient. “Not if you don’t want to. But it might make things easier. For you and for them.”

Reluctantly, Ava followed him out of the mess hall. The hallway felt narrower with him leading the way, his steady pace setting a rhythm she struggled to match. He stopped at a door labeled “Engineering Bay” and tapped the console to open it.

Inside, the space was alive with sound and motion. Crew members bustled between stations, their voices blending with the hum of machinery. Gabriel led her to a woman with cropped hair and grease-smudged hands, bent over a control panel.

“Cora,” Gabriel said, his tone warm. “Got a minute?”

The woman glanced up, her sharp eyes narrowing slightly when they landed on Ava. “What’s this?”

“This is Ava,” Gabriel said. “She’s… new. Thought it’d be good for her to see how things work around here.”

Cora wiped her hands on a rag and straightened. “You’re the one everyone’s been talking about.”

Ava’s stomach tightened. “Probably,” she said quietly.

Cora’s gaze lingered for a moment before she extended a hand. “Cora. Chief Engineer. If Gabriel dragged you here, I’m guessing you’re worth something. He doesn’t waste his time.”

Ava drew in a slow breath, then shook her hand. “Nice to meet you.”

“We’ll see about that,” Cora said with a smirk before turning back to her work.

Gabriel chuckled. “Don’t take it personally. She’s like that with everyone.”

The next stop was the medical bay. The sterile scent hit Ava immediately, sharp and clean, like antiseptic. Gabriel introduced her to Dr. Malik, a tall man with a kind face and a voice so calm it seemed to lower the room’s temperature.

“I’ve read your file,” Malik said after Gabriel’s introduction. “But files only tell part of the story. How are you feeling?”

“Fine,” Ava replied quickly. Too quickly.

Malik’s eyes softened. “Adjusting to a new environment can be challenging. If you need anything—even just someone to talk to—my door is always open.”

“Thanks,” Ava murmured, though the idea of opening up to a stranger felt impossible.

Their final stop was the observation deck. It was empty this time, the vast window offering an uninterrupted view of the stars. Ava let out a breath she hadn’t realized she was holding.

Gabriel leaned against the railing, watching her. “Not so bad, right?”

She gave a small shrug. “I guess.”

“They’re good people,” he said. “Once they get to know you, they’ll come around.”

“What if they don’t?” Ava’s voice was barely above a whisper.

Gabriel pushed off the railing and stood beside her. “Then they’re missing out.”

Ava looked at him, searching for any hint of insincerity. She found none. She felt a quiet curiosity stirring within her, a sense of wonder at his sincerity, a feeling she hadn’t experienced in a long time. It wasn’t a feeling she could name, but it grounded her in the moment, fragile yet profound.

In the mess hall, the tension among the crew was palpable. Gabriel stood at the head of the room, his arms crossed as he addressed the gathered crew.

“We need to settle this,” he said, his voice firm but calm. “Ava’s here, and she’s staying. Whether you like it or not, we’re all on the same ship. We’ve faced worse together than someone trying to find her place.”

A murmur of disagreement rippled through the room. One of the younger crew members, a wiry man named Cole, stepped forward.

“With all due respect, Gabriel, she’s not just someone trying to find her place,” Cole said. “She’s a ticking time bomb. What if she loses control again? What if next time, it’s not just guards at the fortress she takes out? What if it’s us?”

“That’s enough,” Jenna interjected, her tone sharp. She stepped forward, her usual calm demeanor replaced with a rare intensity. “Ava didn’t cause what happened at the fortress. She was overwhelmed and scared, just like anyone would be in her position. Blaming her for the mutations that took over is unfair and short-sighted.”

Cole’s jaw tightened, but before he could respond, another voice spoke up.

“Jenna’s right,” said Rhea, one of Gabriel’s close teammates. “We’ve all made mistakes under pressure. You don’t throw someone out just because they’re struggling.”

“Struggling?” another crew member said, their tone dripping with sarcasm. “She’s not struggling; she’s dangerous. There’s a difference.”

Gabriel raised a hand, silencing the room. “I hear your concerns. All of them. But this isn’t up for debate. Ava stays. End of discussion.”

The group began to disperse, muttering among themselves. Jenna lingered, her gaze meeting Gabriel’s.

“You sure about this?” she asked quietly.

Gabriel sighed. “She deserves a chance, Jenna. Just like anyone else.”

Jenna nodded. “Then we’ll make it work. One way or another.”

Ava sat in the dimly lit cargo hold, her arms wrapped around her knees. The quiet hum of the ship filled the space, a steady rhythm that should have been comforting. But her mind was anything but calm. She replayed the memories of the fortress—the fear, the chaos, the blood. She clenched her fists, her body trembling.

The door to the cargo hold slid open, and Gabriel stepped in, his boots echoing softly on the metal floor. He paused when he saw her, his brow furrowing.

“You haven’t eaten,” he murmured, his voice carrying quiet concern as he took a cautious step closer. His gaze flickered to the untouched meal tray beside her, the food long gone cold. The dim lighting cast shadows across her face, accentuating the exhaustion in her features.

Ava didn’t respond. Her gaze remained fixed on the floor.

Gabriel sighed, sitting down across from her. “You’re shutting yourself out again.”

“I don’t trust myself,” Ava whispered, her voice barely audible.

Gabriel leaned forward, resting his elbows on his knees. “Ava, listen to me. What happened back there wasn’t your fault. You were scared. Anyone would’ve been.”

“I hurt people,” Ava said, her voice breaking. She looked up, her eyes glistening with tears. “I killed them, Gabriel. I became the thing they all fear.”

Gabriel’s expression softened. “You’re not a monster. You’ve saved more lives than you’ve taken. You saved me.”

Ava shook her head, but Gabriel reached out, his hand resting on hers. His grip was warm, steady—an anchor in the storm raging inside her. She tensed at the contact, expecting him to recoil, to hesitate like the others always did. But he didn’t. He held her hand as if it were the most natural thing in the world, his thumb tracing small, absent-minded circles over her knuckles.

“You’re stronger than you think,” he said, his voice steady, unwavering. “And you don’t have to go through this alone.”

The sincerity in his words made something shift within her, something fragile but undeniable. She had spent so long fearing what she was, so long convinced that every path she walked would end in destruction. But here was Gabriel—unafraid, unwavering, choosing to believe in her even when she couldn’t believe in herself.

Her fingers tightened slightly around his, hesitant but real. She could feel the callouses on his palm, the roughness of a man who had fought too many battles and carried too many burdens. And yet, despite it all, he still found the strength to offer her this—this sliver of hope, this quiet reassurance that she wasn’t as lost as she thought she was.

Ava swallowed hard, her throat tightening with emotion. “What if I lose control again?” she whispered, barely able to say the words aloud.

Gabriel didn’t look away. “Then I’ll be here to pull you back.”

Something inside her cracked, a dam she hadn’t realized she had built. It wasn’t much—not yet—but for the first time in what felt like forever, she felt the tiniest spark of courage.

Over the following days, Ava began to focus on small, tangible goals. Gabriel’s words echoed in her mind, and she clung to them like a lifeline. She started by helping Jenna and the crew organize supplies. The repetitive tasks gave her a sense of control, grounding her in the present.

One day, while sorting through seeds salvaged from a scavenging mission, she found herself drawn to the idea of growing something. It was a memory from her past, hazy but persistent—her work as a botanist, nurturing life in a controlled environment.

She approached Gabriel hesitantly. “I want to try building a greenhouse,” she said, her voice uncertain.

Gabriel’s eyes lit up. “That’s a great idea. What do you need?”

Her heart swelled at his encouragement. Together, they began converting the ship’s cargo hold into a makeshift greenhouse.

At first, Ava felt overwhelmed. The project was ambitious, and her confidence wavered. But as the first seedlings sprouted under the grow lights, a sense of accomplishment began to take root.

Gabriel often stopped by, offering help or simply sitting with her while she worked. His presence was a steadying force, and Ava found herself leaning on him less out of necessity and more out of trust.

Jenna, too, began to soften. She watched Ava’s transformation from a distance, her own guilt eating away at her. One day, she approached Ava in the greenhouse, carrying a tray of water bottles.

“You’re really making this place feel alive,” Jenna said, her voice tentative.

Ava looked up, surprised. “I’m just… trying to help.”

Jenna nodded, setting the tray down. “You’re doing more than that. The crew is starting to trust you. I’m starting to trust you.”

Ava smiled faintly, the words lifting a weight from her shoulders.

The survivors, initially wary, began to see Ava in a new light. She worked tirelessly, teaching them how to tend to the plants and explaining the science behind each step. Slowly, their fear turned to curiosity, then respect.

One evening, as Ava watered the rows of leafy greens, Gabriel joined her. He leaned against a nearby support beam, watching her with a mix of admiration and gratitude.

“You’ve changed,” he said after a while.

Ava paused, looking at him. “I just… I wanted to do something good. To prove I’m not…” She trailed off, unable to finish the thought.

Gabriel stepped closer, his voice soft. “You’ve already proved that. To all of us.”

Ava’s eyes glimmered with unshed tears, but this time, they were tears of hope.

Their journey took them to abandoned outposts and forgotten settlements, scavenging for resources to sustain the ship. Each mission was fraught with danger, but the crew grew stronger with each success.

Ava stayed behind during these missions, focusing on her work in the greenhouse. She hated feeling like a bystander, but she couldn’t shake the terror of her own power.

One day, after returning from a particularly perilous scavenging trip, Gabriel handed her a small, weathered journal.

“I thought you might like this,” he said, his voice warm.

Ava opened it, running her fingers over the blank pages. “Thank you,” she said softly.

Gabriel smiled. “You’ve come a long way, Ava. Don’t forget that.”

She bowed her head slightly, clutching the journal tightly. For the first time, she began to imagine a future where she could find her place—not as a monster, but as someone who could bring life and hope to those around her.

Despite the progress, a part of Ava still felt incomplete. Deep down, she knew she was capable of more—of fighting the alien mutation that had destroyed so much. But the fear of losing control again kept her rooted in place.

One night, as the ship glided silently through the stars, Ava sat alone in the greenhouse, staring at the journal Gabriel had given her. She opened it to the first blank page, hesitated, then began to write:

I’m afraid. But I don’t want to be anymore.

Her handwriting wavered, but as she continued, the words came easier. She wrote about her fears, her memories, and the flicker of hope that Gabriel had given her.

Weeks later, as Ava adjusted the position of a sprouting herb, the sound of the door opening broke her focus. She looked up to see Gabriel stepping inside, his presence filling the room with an easy confidence. He carried a small crate in his hands, its contents hidden beneath a cloth.

“Thought I’d find you here,” he said with a grin, setting the crate down on a nearby table. “Brought you something.”

Ava wiped her hands on a rag and stood, curiosity piqued. “What is it?”

Gabriel pulled back the cloth, revealing several small jars of seeds. “Figured you could use a little variety,” he said. “There’s some herbs, a few flowers… and whatever this is.” He held up a jar filled with tiny, irregularly shaped seeds, squinting at the label.

Ava couldn’t help but smile. “Where did you find these?”

“Let’s just say I have my sources,” he replied, winking. “Thought it might help to have something new to work on.”

Her smile softened as she looked at him. “Thank you. This means a lot.”

Gabriel leaned against the table, watching her as she examined the seeds. “You’ve put a lot of work into this place,” he said, his voice thoughtful. “It’s… peaceful. Feels like a piece of home.”

Ava nodded, brushing a strand of hair out of her face. “It’s the one thing I can control,” she admitted. “When everything else feels like it’s falling apart, this is something I can focus on. Something that won’t hurt anyone.”

Gabriel’s expression softened. He stepped closer, his voice quieter now, tinged with warmth. “Ava, you’re allowed to feel like this. You’ve been through so much, and it’s okay to just… be where you are. No expectations.””

She looked up at him, her breath catching at the sincerity in his eyes. For a moment, the world outside the greenhouse seemed to fade away. It was just the two of them, standing amidst the quiet life she had nurtured.

“I don’t feel like I’m in my own body,” she whispered, her voice barely audible.

Gabriel tilted his head, studying her. “Maybe you don’t have to feel like yourself all at once,” he said softly. “Maybe it’s okay to just want to feel normal again.”

Ava blinked, her eyes meeting his. “That’s all I want,” she murmured. “To feel like me again. To not feel like… like everything about me is wrong.”

Gabriel nodded, his voice steady. “You’re not wrong, Ava. And normal? It’s overrated anyway. But I get it. You deserve that peace.”

Ava’s eyes welled with tears, but she blinked them away, focusing on the crate of seeds. “You really didn’t have to do this,” she said, her voice thick with emotion.

“I wanted to,” Gabriel replied simply.

A silence settled between them, comfortable and warm. Ava reached for one of the jars, turning it over in her hands. “Maybe you can help me plant these,” she said, glancing at him.

Gabriel’s grin returned. “Are you sure you trust me with your precious greenhouse?”

“I’ll supervise,” she said with a small laugh.

They worked side by side, Gabriel following Ava’s careful instructions as they planted the new seeds. He was clumsy at first, spilling soil and planting a few seeds too close together, but his good-natured humor lightened the atmosphere. Ava found herself laughing more than she had in weeks, her usual tension melting away.

When they finished, they stood back to admire their work. The rows of newly planted seeds looked almost insignificant, but to Ava, they held the promise of growth and renewal.

“Not bad for your first time,” she teased, nudging Gabriel lightly.

“I aim to impress,” he said, pretending to dust his hands off dramatically.

As they left the greenhouse together, Ava felt a warmth in her chest that had nothing to do with the artificial lights. Gabriel’s presence was a comfort she hadn’t realized she needed, and for the first time in a long while, she allowed herself to belive for something more.
