# Chapter 4: Isolation

When Ava woke again, the world felt smaller—cold, sterile, and suffocating. Her body ached from head to toe, every movement a reminder of the invasive tests she’d endured. Blinking through the fog of exhaustion, she became aware of the new space around her. It was no longer the polished steel room where they’d strapped her down. Instead, she found herself in a transparent chamber, its walls made of reinforced glass.

Outside the chamber, figures moved with deliberate purpose, all clad in the same hazmat suits she’d seen earlier. Monitors lined the walls, displaying streams of data she couldn’t comprehend. A low hum filled the air, punctuated by the occasional mechanical hiss of ventilators.

She pushed herself upright, wincing at the stiffness in her limbs. Her clothes had been replaced with a plain, white hospital gown that did little to shield her from the chill of the chamber. She scanned the room beyond, her eyes narrowing as they settled on the scientists observing her. Their detached stares through their visors made her feel like an animal in a cage.

Ava pressed her palm against the glass. “What is this? Where am I?” she demanded, her voice hoarse. “Let me out!”

No one responded. One of the figures—a woman, judging by her frame—briefly glanced at Ava before turning back to her tablet. Ava slammed her fist against the glass, but it didn’t even shudder under the force.

The intercom crackled to life, and a calm, clinical voice filled the chamber. “Subject 017, remain calm. Your elevated heart rate is being monitored.”

“Subject?” Ava spat, her anger bubbling to the surface. “I have a name. I’m a person!”

There was no reply. The intercom went silent, leaving Ava alone with her frustration and fear.

Her breathing quickened as she backed away from the glass, her legs trembling. The events of the past hours—or was it days? she couldn’t tell—played on a loop in her mind. The abduction. The tests. The burning liquid coursing through her veins. She glanced down at her arms, expecting to see bruises or needle marks, but instead found something far stranger. The faint black veins she’d noticed in the hospital had grown darker, more pronounced, snaking up her forearms like invasive roots.

Ava’s stomach churned. She touched the veins tentatively, her fingertips brushing over her clammy skin. They felt warm, almost alive.

“What are you doing to me?” she whispered, her voice trembling.

The scientists outside seemed oblivious to her distress, continuing their work as if she weren’t there. One of them approached a control panel beside her chamber, their gloved hands flipping switches and adjusting dials. The hum in the room grew louder, and a faint mist began to fill the chamber.

Ava coughed, waving her hand in front of her face as the mist thickened. It smelled faintly metallic, and her lungs burned with each breath. She stumbled back, her body hitting the glass wall.

Through the haze, she saw the scientists watching her intently, their visors reflecting the dim light. The woman with the tablet leaned closer to the intercom. “Initiating spore exposure. Subject’s vitals are stable.”

“Spore exposure?” Ava gasped, coughing violently. Her mind reeled. The spores they’d mentioned earlier—the ones tied to the meteorite—were here, in this mist. She could feel them invading her lungs, clawing their way into her body.

Her vision blurred as her body convulsed. She fell to her knees, clutching her chest as the pain spread like wildfire. The black veins on her arms pulsed, glowing faintly with an unnatural light. Ava screamed, her voice raw, as the sensation intensified. She felt her body changing, her cells tearing themselves apart and knitting back together in a grotesque cycle.

Then, as suddenly as it had begun, the pain stopped. Ava collapsed onto the floor, her body trembling. The mist began to dissipate, sucked away by unseen vents. She lay there, gasping for air, her sweat-soaked hair clinging to her face.

The intercom crackled again. “Subject 017’s resistance levels are remarkable,” the same voice said, this time with a note of fascination. “Prepare for phase two.”

Ava’s fingers curled into fists as she fought the wave of nausea and dread. She wasn’t sure what “phase two” meant, but she knew one thing for certain: she couldn’t let them keep doing this to her. Somehow, she had to find a way to escape.
