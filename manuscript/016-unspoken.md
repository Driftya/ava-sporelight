# Chapter 16: Unspoken

Ava sat on the edge of her makeshift bed in the greenhouse, staring at the tiny blossoms beginning to sprout from one of the plants she’d nurtured for weeks. There was something magical about the way life could emerge from nothing, something fragile yet resilient. It reminded her of how she felt around Gabriel—unsure and timid, but oddly hopeful.

She traced her fingers over the edge of the journal he’d given her, a small smile tugging at her lips. His kindness had been constant, his presence a quiet anchor. But with every kind word, every lingering glance, she felt the stirrings of something unfamiliar.

Ava sighed, closing the journal and setting it aside. The very thought of her feelings made her stomach twist. How could someone like Gabriel see her as anything other than the monster she feared she was?

Gabriel, meanwhile, was pacing the ship’s main corridor, his mind replaying moments from the past few weeks. Ava’s transformation had been extraordinary—not just in the way she was building something beautiful from the chaos, but in the way she carried herself.

Her smile had become more frequent, her voice less hesitant. And there were moments, fleeting but unforgettable, when he caught a glimpse of the woman she might have been before all of this. He couldn’t deny she was stunning, though he’d never say it aloud—not yet, at least.

Still, he couldn’t shake the feeling that now wasn’t the time. With the survivors relying on them, the constant threat of alien mutations, and Ava’s lingering self-doubt, he knew any step toward a relationship would complicate things.

One afternoon, Ava found herself wandering into the ship’s small medical bay where Jenna was sorting supplies. Jenna glanced up, raising an eyebrow. “Need something?”

Ava hesitated, fiddling with the edge of her sleeve. “Not… really. Just wanted to talk.”

Jenna leaned back against the counter, crossing her arms. “Talk, huh? That’s new for you. What’s on your mind?”

Ava bit her lip, her gaze darting to the floor. “I… I’ve been thinking about Gabriel. About how he’s always… there.”

Jenna’s expression softened. “He’s a good guy.”

“I know,” Ava said quickly. “That’s part of the problem. He’s good, and I’m… not.”

Jenna sighed, stepping closer, her expression no longer one of a detached scientist but of someone who understood far more than she let on. “Ava, listen to me. I’ve watched you over these past weeks, and you’re not the same person who nearly tore me apart in that quarantine room.” She hesitated, then let out a small, rueful laugh. “But I don’t think I ever really saw you back then. Not as a person. I was too caught up in what you could do… and too scared of what that meant.”

Ava’s lips parted slightly, but she said nothing. A sharp, hollow ache bloomed in her chest, the memory of those first few days—cold, clinical, filled with eyes that studied her instead of seeing her—still fresh beneath the surface.

Jenna’s gaze softened, and she rubbed a hand across the back of her neck, as if trying to ease the weight of her own words. “I should have treated you differently. I should have tried to understand, instead of just fearing what I didn’t know. And I’m sorry again, Ava. Truly.”

The words settled heavily between them, and Ava wasn’t sure how to respond. She had spent so long carrying that fear, believing that was all people would ever see in her. To hear Jenna admit she had been wrong, to hear an apology from someone who once looked at her like a caged experiment—it was unexpected. And it hurt more than she thought it would.

“I don’t blame you,” Ava murmured, though a part of her wasn’t sure if that was entirely true. “I was scared, too.”

Jenna nodded, her lips pressing into a thin line. “I know. And I won’t pretend to understand what that was like for you. But I see you now, Ava. And I see how hard you’re trying.” She exhaled, her voice turning gentle. “That counts for a lot.”

Ava swallowed, the words sticking in her throat. “But it’s not just that,” she admitted, lowering her gaze. “I don’t even know if he could ever see me the way I… I think about him.” Her fingers curled into the fabric of her sleeves. “And even if he did, what happens when I lose control again? What if I hurt him?”

Jenna was quiet for a long moment. Then, carefully, she said, “You’re scared. And that’s normal. But you don’t have to figure everything out right now.”

Ava’s jaw clenched. “But what if I ruin everything? What if I ruin him?”

Jenna’s eyes flickered with something unreadable, and she hesitated, her next words slower, more deliberate. “You care about him, don’t you?”

Ava’s breath hitched slightly. “Of course I do.”

“Then trust him,” Jenna said simply. “Trust that if this—whatever this is—matters to him, he won’t run just because you’re afraid.”

Ava shook her head. “It’s not that simple.”

“It never is.” Jenna offered a small, knowing smile. “But you don’t have to carry all of this alone. Gabriel’s stronger than you think. And so are you.”

Ava glanced away, her fingers tightening around her sleeve again, as if she could steady herself with just that small, familiar pressure. “I just… I don’t know how to stop being afraid.”

Jenna sighed, then reached out, placing a hand lightly on Ava’s shoulder. “You don’t,” she said quietly. “You learn how to live with it. And you let the people who care about you help.”

Ava swallowed hard. The weight in her chest didn’t disappear, but for the first time, it shifted—just enough to let her breathe.

She nodded, though her doubts still lingered.

Later that evening, Gabriel found Ava in the greenhouse. She was kneeling by a row of plants, her hands gently brushing over the leaves. She didn’t notice him at first, and he stood there for a moment, watching her.

The way the light from the grow lamps framed her face made his chest tighten. She looked so peaceful, so focused. He caught himself staring and shook his head, stepping forward.

“Need a hand?” he asked, his voice breaking the quiet.

Ava looked up, startled, but her lips curved into a soft smile. “Sure. I was just checking for pests.”

Gabriel crouched beside her, glancing at the plants. “You’ve turned this place into something incredible, you know.”

She ducked her head, a faint blush creeping into her cheeks. “It’s nothing, really. Just… what I know how to do.”

“It’s not nothing,” Gabriel said firmly. “You’ve given everyone hope, Ava. Including me.”

Her breath hitched, and for a moment, their eyes met. She quickly looked away, her heart pounding.

“Thank you,” she whispered.

They worked in silence for a while, the unspoken tension hanging between them. Gabriel wanted to say more, but every time he tried to find the words, he stopped himself. Now wasn’t the time.

That night, as Ava lay in her bed, she found herself replaying their conversation. She pressed her hand to her chest, feeling the rapid beat of her heart.

She didn’t know if Gabriel felt the same, but for the first time, she allowed herself to imagine a future beyond fear .

Meanwhile, in his own quarters, Gabriel sat on the edge of his bunk, staring at the floor. He thought of Ava—the way she smiled, the way she worked tirelessly to help everyone, the quiet strength she didn’t seem to realize she had.

He sighed, running a hand through his hair. “Not now,” he muttered to himself. “It’s not the right time.”

But deep down, he knew he couldn’t ignore the way she made him feel for much longer.

It wasn’t just the unspoken moments that lingered between them—sometimes, the unexpected ones left a deeper mark. One particularly awkward moment still burned fresh in both their minds. It had been late, and Gabriel, assuming her room was empty, had stepped in without knocking. He froze in place when he realized his mistake, his face turning crimson as Ava quickly scrambled to cover herself. His startled expression and immediate retreat had been almost comical, as he muttered a string of apologies before disappearing out the door. Ava, meanwhile, had been left standing there, her face aflame as she hurriedly dressed. By the time she called after him—her voice a mix of embarrassment and frustration—she could barely get the words out. “Next time, knock, you idiot!” she had managed, her tone more flustered than angry. Later, when their paths crossed in the hallway, neither had the courage to address it directly. Gabriel’s sheepish grin and averted gaze were met with Ava’s faint blush and a quick, muttered “It’s fine.” The incident, though fleeting, had somehow eased the weight of their interactions, breaking through the tension with a dose of awkward, all-too-human reality. It was a reminder of the vulnerabilities they both carried, and for a brief moment, the chaos outside felt a little less daunting.

The greenhouse was filled with the soft rustle of leaves as Ava carefully tended to her plants, her mind wandering more than usual. Gabriel had been out on an errand—or maybe a mission; she wasn’t entirely sure. All she knew was that he wasn’t there, and his absence left an ache she couldn’t quite shake. The space had become a reflection of her, each corner carrying her meticulous touch. But today, her movements were slower, her mind preoccupied with thoughts she couldn’t quite silence.

She hadn’t seen Gabriel all morning, and the absence gnawed at her more than she cared to admit. She found herself wondering what he was doing, what he was thinking. His voice had a way of cutting through the noise in her head, grounding her in a way nothing else could. And she missed it.

When the door to the greenhouse slid open, Ava turned quickly, her heart skipping a beat. For a fleeting moment, she thought it was Gabriel, and her breath caught in her throat. But instead, it was Marcus, one of the younger crew members. He stepped inside with a friendly smile, carrying a small crate of supplies. Ava’s anticipation faded into a polite smile, tinged with disappointment she hoped wasn’t obvious.

“Hey, Ava,” Marcus said, setting the crate down on the table. “I thought you might need these. Some extra supplies for the greenhouse.” He hesitated, scratching the back of his neck. “Gabriel mentioned you’ve been putting a lot of work into this place.”

Ava gave him a small smile, though her thoughts still lingered on Gabriel. “That’s thoughtful, Marcus. Thanks.”

“You didn’t have to bring all this,” Ava said, glancing at the crate. For a moment, she seemed caught between words before adding, “Do you know if Gabriel is back yet?”

Marcus shook his head. “Not yet. He’s probably still out on that supply run. It’s been a while, huh?” He smiled awkwardly. “But hey, he’ll be back soon.”

Ava hesitated, then asked, “Have you known Gabriel for a long time?”

Marcus leaned against the table, his expression thoughtful. “A few years, give or take. He’s the kind of guy who leaves an impression, you know? Always looking out for people.”

“That sounds like him,” Ava said softly, her fingers brushing over a leaf absently. “Has he ever talked about why he joined the rescue squad?”

Marcus shrugged. “A little. I mean, not in detail. He’s not the type to go on about himself. But from what I gather, he’s seen a lot—probably more than most of us. Whatever brought him here, it’s personal.”

Ava nodded, filing away Marcus’s words. She didn’t want to push too much; hearing these things from Gabriel himself mattered more. “Thanks for sharing, Marcus.”

“Anytime,” he replied with a grin. “You know, you’re kind of awesome, Ava. Not many people can say they can fight off those alien things the way you can. It’s like you’ve got abilities the rest of us can’t even imagine.”

Ava blinked, caught off guard. “Abilities?” she echoed, her tone a mix of surprise and awkwardness.

Marcus nodded enthusiastically. “Seriously! I mean, you saved Gabriel, right? You’ve got abilities that could save lives. That’s pretty incredible.”

Ava’s cheeks warmed, and she looked down at her hands, brushing invisible dirt from her palms. “I don’t know about that,” she murmured. “It’s not exactly something I think of as… special.”

“Well, I think it is,” Marcus said, his tone light but sincere. “And, you know, it’s nice to have someone like you around. You’re not just strong; you’re also really easy to talk to.”

Ava’s lips curved into a small smile, though she felt a twinge of awkwardness. “Thanks, Marcus. That means a lot.”

The air in the greenhouse was warm, the faint scent of soil and leaves wrapping around Ava like a comforting embrace. She stood at the far table, carefully inspecting the new seedlings that had begun to sprout. Her hands worked methodically, yet her mind was far from the tasks at hand.

Gabriel would be back tomorrow.

The news had sent a ripple of energy through her, though she tried to contain it. She had learned of his return in passing, a casual remark from one of the crew, but it was enough to set her thoughts racing. She found herself yearning to see him again, to hear his steady voice that always seemed to anchor her when everything else felt unsteady.

Ava let out a slow breath, brushing her fingers over the delicate leaves of a sprout. She could barely admit it to herself, but just the thought of him made her chest tighten. There was something about Gabriel—his calm demeanor, the way he listened without judgment, his unwavering presence—that drew her in. She wanted to know more, to peel back the layers he so carefully kept guarded.

But she also knew that waiting was all she could do for now.

Later that evening, Ava sat cross-legged on the floor of her quarters, a small lamp casting a soft glow over the room. The journal Gabriel had given her lay open in her lap, but the words blurred together as her thoughts drifted.

She wondered what Gabriel’s life had been like before the rescue squad. Marcus had mentioned that Gabriel wasn’t one to talk much about himself, which only made Ava more curious. She wondered what kind of experiences had shaped him, what memories he held close but rarely shared. The way he carried himself—calm and steady, yet with an edge of something unspoken—hinted at a depth she longed to understand.

“What drives you, Gabriel?” she murmured to herself, closing the journal gently. “What brought you here?”

She leaned back against the wall, her eyes drifting to the ceiling. The ache of his absence was tangible, but it was more than just missing his presence. It was the not knowing that gnawed at her. What had shaped the man who had saved her and stood by her side when no one else dared to?

The next morning, Ava woke early, unable to stay in bed any longer. She found herself back in the greenhouse, her hands busy with tasks that didn’t truly need doing. Every sound outside the room made her heart leap, only to sink when it wasn’t him.

She thought back to what Marcus had said the day before. “You saved Gabriel, right?” The memory brought a faint warmth to her cheeks. She hadn’t thought much of her actions at the time—it had been instinct, a desperate attempt to survive. But hearing it framed that way made her feel something she hadn’t expected: pride.

And yet, it was tempered by the weight of what her abilities cost her. Saving people wasn’t simple or easy; it came with pain, fear, and the constant reminder of what she was. She only hoped that one day Gabriel might see her for more than just her abilities. She wanted him to know her—the real her, the parts she kept hidden even from herself.

By midday, Ava found herself in the common area, seated near a viewport that stretched across the wall. The stars beyond glittered like shards of broken glass, and she let her gaze wander over them, her thoughts as scattered as the light.

Tomorrow couldn’t come fast enough.

She thought of Gabriel’s voice, the way it carried a calm confidence that made her feel like everything might just be okay. She imagined him walking through the doors, his easy smile lighting up the room, and the thought made her heart ache in a way she couldn’t fully understand.

“Just one more day,” she whispered, her breath fogging the glass. “I can wait one more day.”

The soft murmur of voices from the docking bay carried through the ship, and Ava’s heart raced as she stood by the greenhouse door. Gabriel was back. She hadn’t seen him yet, but the buzz of the crew confirmed his return.

She wiped her hands on her apron, suddenly feeling self-conscious about the dirt smudged across her fingers. A part of her wanted to wait, to let him find her, but her legs carried her toward the docking bay before she could second-guess herself.

When she arrived, the bay was bustling with activity. Supplies were being unloaded, and crew members moved in organized chaos. Ava glanced around the room, her eyes searching for him. And then, there he was, standing near one of the crates, his back to her as he spoke to another crew member. His presence was like a beacon, and Ava felt a warmth spread through her chest.

She hesitated, unsure whether to interrupt. But as if sensing her, Gabriel turned, his gaze locking onto hers. A smile broke across his face, one that reached his eyes and made the corners crinkle slightly. It was the kind of smile that made her breath catch.

“Ava,” he said, his voice steady and familiar, a sound she hadn’t realized how much she’d missed until now.

“Welcome back,” she said softly, her lips curving into a smile of her own.

Gabriel closed the distance between them in a few strides, his presence filling the space between them. “It’s good to be back,” he said. “How’ve you been?”

Ava hesitated, unsure how to condense the whirlwind of emotions she’d been feeling. “Busy,” she said simply, her voice quieter than she intended. “And you? How was the mission?”

“Straightforward,” Gabriel replied. “We got what we needed and avoided trouble for once.”

She nodded, relief washing over her. “I’m glad.”

Later that evening, Gabriel found her in the greenhouse. The room was dimly lit, the glow from the grow lights casting a warm hue over the plants. Ava was crouched near the far table, tending to a set of new sprouts. She looked up as he entered, brushing a strand of hair from her face.

“I figured you’d be here,” he said, his voice warm as he leaned casually against the doorframe, a hint of a smile on his lips.

Ava smiled, though there was a nervous energy in her movements as she straightened. “It’s my favorite place,” she admitted. “Helps me think.”

Gabriel stepped further into the room, his eyes taking in the flourishing plants. “You’ve done a lot with this place. It feels alive in here.”

Ava glanced around, her expression softening. “It’s my way of bringing a little life back to things.”

They stood in silence for a moment, the weight of unspoken words settling between them. Finally, Gabriel broke the silence.

“I’ve been thinking about the questions you’ve been asking,” he began, his tone thoughtful but cautious. “Marcus might’ve mentioned you’ve been curious… asking about me.”

Ava’s chest tightened, and a flush crept up her neck. “I didn’t mean to pry,” she said softly, her voice laced with a mix of nervousness and apology.

Gabriel shook his head, a faint smile tugging at his lips. “It’s not that. It’s just… talking about what shaped me isn’t something I do often. But with you, it feels different. Safer. And I think it might help.”

He paused, his gaze dropping slightly as he seemed to gather his thoughts. “Sometimes it feels like their memory is all I have left of them. And it’s heavy, Ava. Like I’m constantly balancing it, afraid that if I stumble, I’ll lose them all over again.”

“You don’t have to carry that alone,” Ava said softly. “They’d be proud of you, Gabriel. Of what you’ve done.”

His gaze met hers, and for a moment, the vulnerability in his eyes made her breath catch. “Thank you,” he said. “It’s not easy to open up, but with you… it feels different. Safer.”

Ava’s cheeks warmed, and she looked down at the table, brushing her fingers over a stray leaf. “I’m glad,” she murmured.

Gabriel stepped closer, the distance between them shrinking. "You know, Marcus mentioned something funny earlier," he began, his voice softer now. "He said that every time you talk to him, it always comes back to me."

Ava’s face flushed, and she looked down, fiddling with a stray leaf on the table. "I… I didn’t mean for it to seem like that," she said hesitantly. "I guess… I just think about you more than I realize."

Gabriel’s smile softened, and he tilted his head slightly, studying her. "You know, Marcus isn’t wrong," he said, his tone more reflective now. "It means a lot to know someone’s thinking about you. And Ava, I’m glad it’s you."

Ava hesitated, her gaze dropping for a moment before she spoke. "You’re lucky to have memories of your parents," she said softly. "I don’t remember mine. Not really. I try sometimes, but it’s all fragments, like pieces of a puzzle I can’t put together."

Gabriel’s expression shifted, his brows knitting together as he stepped closer. "That must be really hard," he said, his voice low with empathy. "But memories can be tricky. They have a way of finding us when we least expect it."

Her eyes met his, glistening with unshed tears. "It’s just… I wonder what they must have felt, Gabriel," she said, her voice trembling. "Their child went missing without a trace. Did they spend the rest of their lives searching for answers? Did they think I was still out there?" She paused, her voice dropping to a whisper. "I’ll never know if they had hope or if they ever forgave themselves for losing me."

Gabriel exhaled slowly, his voice low and filled with emotion. "I can’t imagine what that’s like, but I do know this: whoever they were, they’d see someone worth being proud of."

Ava’s throat tightened, and she gave a small nod, unable to speak. Gabriel reached out, brushing her arm lightly in a gesture of comfort. "You’re not alone in this, Ava," he said softly, his voice filled with quiet conviction. "Whatever comes, you have someone who’ll stand beside you."

Ava felt a warmth spread through her chest, a mix of gratitude and something she couldn’t quite name. For the first time, she let herself believe it might be true. She didn’t have to face everything alone. Not anymore.

The air carried a charged stillness, as though the ship itself was holding its breath. Ava stood in one of the storage rooms, her fingers brushing over the neatly stacked containers as she moved slowly through the space. The quiet order of the room, with its rows of labeled supplies, gave her a strange sense of comfort. The words Gabriel had shared last night lingered in her mind, intertwining with the constant pulse of the ship. Ava walked through the dimly lit corridors, her mind restless despite the calm around her. She had come here hoping to find a distraction, but her thoughts kept circling back to Gabriel and the way he had opened up to her the night before.

As the minutes ticked by, Ava wandered from one corridor to the next, her footsteps aimless until she found herself at the observation deck. The wide expanse of the viewport always seemed to pull her in when her thoughts grew heavy. They shimmered against the dark void, a reminder of how vast the universe was and how small her place in it could feel. Yet, tonight, that vastness didn’t overwhelm her. It felt like a promise—one she wasn’t sure she understood yet but wanted to.

As she pressed her palm against the glass, the stars beyond seemed to pulse faintly, a quiet reminder of the vast, unpredictable universe waiting beyond the ship. Her mind replayed Gabriel’s words from the previous night, the softness in his voice, the way he seemed to let his guard down just for her. She thought about her parents, about what they might have thought if they had known what their daughter would become. Her chest tightened at the thought.

“Couldn’t sleep either?”

Gabriel’s voice broke through her thoughts, and she turned to see him standing in the doorway. He was dressed casually, his stance relaxed but his eyes searching hers.

Ava smiled faintly. “Seems like this is the place to think,” she said, gesturing toward the viewport.

He stepped closer, his presence steady and comforting. “What’s on your mind?”

She hesitated, her fingers brushing against the glass. “Everything,” she admitted. “The past, the future… everything in between.”

Gabriel nodded, his gaze shifting to the stars. “It’s a lot to carry.”

“It is,” Ava said softly. She glanced at him, her voice tentative. “Do you ever wonder if we’re heading toward something we can’t stop? Something bigger than us?”

He was quiet for a moment, his expression thoughtful. “Sometimes,” he said. “But I think about the people we’ve helped, the lives we’ve saved. That has to count for something, doesn’t it?”

Ava’s lips curved into a small smile. “It does,” she said, her voice steadier. “It’s what keeps me going.”

Gabriel turned to face her fully, his expression soft but serious. “Whatever’s out there, whatever we’re heading toward, we’ll face it.”

She looked at him, the weight of his words settling over her. For a moment, she let herself believe it, allowed the warmth of his presence to fill the empty spaces inside her. “Together,” she echoed.

They stood in silence, the stars stretching out before them like a map of endless possibilities. Ava felt the tension in her chest ease slightly, replaced by a quiet determination she hadn’t felt in years.

As they left the observation deck, walking side by side, Ava glanced at Gabriel. “Do you think we’ll ever find it? What we’re searching for?”

He smiled faintly, his gaze distant. “I think we’ll find something. Maybe not what we’re expecting, but something worth fighting for.”

Ava nodded, her steps lighter. She didn’t know what tomorrow would bring, but for the first time in a long while, she felt ready to face it.

In the quiet of her quarters, Ava lay awake, staring at the ceiling. The words they had exchanged replayed in her mind, a steady rhythm that calmed her restless thoughts. She closed her eyes, a small smile tugging at her lips as she whispered to herself.

“Tomorrow is waiting.”
