# Chapter 14: The Escape

The fortress groaned around them, the once-solid walls now crumbling under the weight of chaos. Alarms blared intermittently, a stark reminder of the outbreak’s devastation. Gabriel and Ava moved quickly through the corridors, their breaths heavy in the freezing air.

“Grab anything useful,” Gabriel instructed, stopping at a supply room. “We don’t know what’s left outside.”

Ava nodded, pulling open cabinets and stuffing medical kits, food rations, and water containers into a bag. Gabriel secured a small oxygen tank and a thermal blanket. “Let’s go,” he urged, slinging the supplies over his shoulder.

The pair made their way toward the dock, their footsteps echoing ominously. Every shadow felt like a potential threat. Ava couldn’t help but glance back occasionally, her unease growing with each step.

“We’re almost there,” Gabriel muttered, his jaw clenched as his eyes swept the path ahead. The tension in his stance was unmistakable—he was ready for a fight, expecting trouble before they even arrived.

When they reached the dock, it was a scene of destruction. Broken crates and abandoned vehicles were scattered everywhere. Several transport vehicles were destroyed, their frames twisted and charred. Gabriel scanned the area desperately.

“There!” Ava pointed toward a small transport vehicle tucked into the corner.

They hurried over to it, Gabriel quickly inspecting the exterior. “It looks functional enough,” he muttered, climbing into the driver’s seat. He powered it up, relief washing over him as the engine roared to life. “Get in!”

Ava climbed into the passenger seat, clutching her bag of supplies tightly. Gabriel maneuvered the vehicle out of the dock, heading toward the frozen plains beyond the fortress.

Meanwhile, aboard the ship they had arrived in, Jenna sat on the bridge, her fingers tapping nervously against the console. She had managed to escape with a small group of survivors, all of whom were now huddled in the ship’s lower quarters.

Her mind was plagued with guilt. She had followed protocol, leaving Gabriel and Ava behind in the fortress. It had been the logical choice, but it didn’t feel right.

“Commander,” a voice broke through her thoughts. A young medic approached, his expression anxious. “What’s the plan?”

Jenna sighed. “We hold our position for now. We can’t risk moving until we know the situation outside has stabilized.”

The medic hesitated. “Do you think they’re… still alive?”

Jenna looked away, unable to answer.

The transport vehicle bumped over uneven terrain, the harsh wind whipping against its sides. Inside, the tension between Gabriel and Ava was palpable.

“What’s on your mind?” Gabriel asked, his eyes on the road ahead.

Ava hesitated, staring out the window. “Do you think anyone else made it out?”

Gabriel glanced at her. “Jenna’s resourceful. If anyone could get people out safely, it’s her.”

“And us?” Ava asked, her voice quieter.

Gabriel gripped the steering wheel tightly. “We’ll make it.”

Hours passed in relative silence, broken only by the hum of the engine. As they approached the frozen plains, Gabriel spotted the ship on the horizon, its exterior gleaming faintly in the distance. Relief washed over him.

“We’re close,” he said.

Ava sat up straighter, her expression a mix of hope and apprehension.

On the ship, Jenna’s monitor beeped, signaling movement outside. She leaned forward, her heart pounding as the screen displayed the transport vehicle approaching.

“It’s them,” she whispered, her voice trembling.

She immediately dispatched a team to guide them into the docking bay. Survivors gathered near the airlock, their murmurs filled with uncertainty and fear.

When the transport docked and Gabriel and Ava stepped out, the tension in the air was almost tangible.

“Gabriel!” Jenna called, rushing toward him.

“Jenna,” Gabriel breathed, tension still laced in his words. “You’re alive.”

A shaky exhale escaped Jenna’s lips as she gave a subtle tilt of her head, as if trying to convince herself it was real. “So are you,” she replied, relief evident in her face, though exhaustion dulled her features. Her gaze drifted past him to Ava, who stood slightly behind, shoulders stiff, her wary eyes shifting between them.

For a brief moment, Jenna hesitated. There was something guarded about Ava’s stance—not quite fear, but the kind of tension Jenna had seen before in survivors who had endured too much, too quickly.

Gabriel’s expression darkened. “You left us.”

Jenna faltered, guilt flickering across her face. “I followed protocol,” she admitted, her voice quieter now. “I didn’t think you’d make it.”

Gabriel shook his head, frustration tightening his jaw. “And that was it? You didn’t even try to find out? We could’ve been alive, trapped, needing help—but you just left.” His voice hardened, though he fought to keep his anger in check. “Protocol or not, you made that call without knowing for sure.”

Jenna looked away, her fingers tightening at her sides.

The survivors murmured among themselves, uneasy glances darting toward Ava. Whispers of uncertainty spread through the group, hushed but unmistakable. Some clutched their weapons a little tighter, while others instinctively put distance between themselves and her.

“She’s dangerous,” one of them muttered, his voice laced with fear. “I saw what she did back there. That thing touched her, and it just—died. No spores, no struggle. Just gone.”

“She walked through them like they weren’t even a threat,” another added, shaking his head. “What kind of person can do that?”

“She’s not like us,” a woman near the back said warily. “And if she turns on us? Who’s going to stop her?”

Gabriel’s jaw tightened. “You don’t know that,” he snapped, his frustration boiling over. “If it weren’t for her, none of us would even be here!”

The crowd fell silent, but the fear in their eyes didn’t fade. It wasn’t just what Ava had done—it was that they had no explanation for it. In a world where survival hinged on understanding the threat, she was an anomaly. And anomalies were dangerous.

Jenna stepped forward, her expression softening. “Gabriel, I—”

“Not now,” Gabriel cut in sharply, his voice brooking no argument.

Later that night, Gabriel sat with Ava in the cargo hold, away from the others. She hugged her knees to her chest, her expression distant.

“I don’t belong here,” Ava said quietly.

Gabriel frowned. “That’s not true.”

“Isn’t it?” Ava looked up at him, her eyes shimmering with unshed tears. “They’re right to be afraid of me.”

“They don’t understand,” Gabriel said firmly. “You’ve done more to protect them than anyone else here.”

Ava shook her head. “All I’ve ever done is hurt people.”

Gabriel leaned closer, his voice softening. “You’ve saved people too. You saved me.”

Ava didn’t respond, but the faintest flicker of hope crossed her face.
