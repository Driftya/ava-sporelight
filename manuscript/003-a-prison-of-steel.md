# Chapter 3: A Prison of Steel

Ava’s consciousness returned in fragments, each one more disorienting than the last. The cold, metallic surface beneath her cheek. The harsh glare of fluorescent lights overhead. The sharp, clinical smell of disinfectant that stung her nose. Her body felt heavy, as if it were weighted down by invisible chains.

She opened her eyes fully, her vision swimming. Blinking several times, she focused on her surroundings. The room was stark and unnervingly pristine, a seamless combination of white walls and polished steel. It felt more like a containment unit than a place of healing, devoid of warmth or humanity. To her left, a bank of machines hummed softly, their blinking lights casting a rhythm of shadows on the floor. Her arms and legs were strapped down to a medical table, her wrists bound by heavy restraints, the cold metal biting into her skin.

Ava’s pulse thundered in her ears as she yanked against the restraints, the unyielding straps biting into her skin. A cold sweat slicked her forehead, her chest rising and falling in uneven, frantic breaths. The sterile scent of antiseptic clung to the air, sharp and suffocating. She twisted her wrists, the bindings refusing to give even an inch.

"Let me out!" she cried, her voice raw with desperation, ricocheting off the stark metal walls. "What is this? Who are you people?"

Her throat tightened, the words tasting like iron and fear. The only response was the rhythmic hum of machines, their blinking lights indifferent to her struggle. Beyond the glass walls, shadowed figures loomed, watching in silence. The weight of their gaze sent a fresh wave of dread crawling up her spine.

Ava’s mind raced, replaying the last moments she remembered. The hospital room. The masked intruders. The prick of the syringe. Her heart sank as she realized she was no longer in the care of doctors. Wherever she was now, it was nothing like the artificial safety of the hospital. Gone were the soft linens and the steady beeping of monitored vitals. Instead, the air here carried a hollow chill, thick with the scent of antiseptic and something darker—metal, sweat, the lingering trace of desperation. The dim, unfeeling lights overhead cast harsh shadows, stretching unnaturally across the smooth, featureless walls. Every surface gleamed with an unsettling precision, as if designed not for healing, but for control.

The door to the room hissed open, and two figures entered, both clad in full hazmat suits that gleamed under the fluorescent lights. One was a tall man with sharp features and cold, calculating eyes. The other, a woman with tightly pulled-back hair, carried a clipboard and tablet. Through the transparent visors of their helmets, their cold, detached eyes regarded Ava not as a person, but as a specimen.

“Subject is awake,” the woman said, not bothering to meet Ava’s frantic gaze. She tapped a few notes into her tablet before glancing at the man. “Vitals are stable. Shall we proceed?”

The man nodded, stepping closer to the table. He reached out, placing a heavily gloved hand on Ava’s arm, ignoring the way she flinched at his touch. “You’ve been selected for an important study,” he said, his voice devoid of emotion. “Your cooperation is… irrelevant. But your survival is critical.”

Ava’s eyes widened in shock. “What are you talking about? Let me go!”

He ignored her outburst, motioning to the woman, who handed him a syringe filled with a glowing green liquid. “Begin with regenerative stress testing,” he instructed. “We’ll need to establish her baseline before introducing the spores.”

The words sent a chill down Ava’s spine. “Spores? What spores? What are you doing to me?” Her voice cracked with panic, but the scientists remained unaffected.

“Administering dose,” the woman stated flatly. She stepped closer, reaching for Ava’s arm. Ava thrashed against the restraints, her muscles straining, but the straps held firm. The needle pierced her skin, and the liquid burned as it entered her bloodstream.

Ava bit back a scream, her body convulsing against the table. Her vision blurred as pain radiated through her veins, hot and searing, like molten metal coursing through her. She gasped, her back arching involuntarily.

“Subject response noted,” the woman said, tapping on her tablet. “Heart rate elevated, cellular activity spiking.”

Through the haze of agony, Ava managed to spit out, “You’re killing me.”

The man leaned over her, his expression unreadable. “No,” he said coldly. “We’re learning.”

As the pain ebbed slightly, Ava’s body went limp, her breaths shallow. Sweat drenched her forehead, and she could feel her pulse hammering in her ears. She barely noticed when the restraints were released, her arms and legs too weak to move.

“Begin spore exposure in the isolation chamber,” the man instructed, stepping back. “Prepare her for the next phase.”

Ava’s head lolled to the side as they wheeled her table toward the door. Her mind raced, grasping for a way out, but her body—broken and battered—refused to cooperate. All she could do was watch as the stark white walls blurred past her, their rigid, antiseptic perfection a chilling contrast to the chaos building inside her. The smooth surfaces, untouched by time or mercy, gleamed under the harsh fluorescent lights, indifferent to her silent screams.

Her final thought before darkness crept in again was simple and terrifying: What have they done to me?
