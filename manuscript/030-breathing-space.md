# Chapter 30: Breathing Space

The hum of the transport vehicle was the only sound for miles, its armored frame shielding the survivors from the chaos they had left behind. Inside, the tension was palpable. The survivors huddled together, whispering quietly, their eyes darting nervously to the windows as if expecting the mutants to burst through at any moment.

Ava sat near the back, her blade resting on her lap. She stared at it, her thoughts a whirl of guilt and exhaustion. The amber veins on her arms had faded, but the memory of her transformation lingered. She flexed her fingers, her body still aching from the strain.

Gabriel lowered himself onto the bench beside her, his rifle resting across his knees. He didn’t speak immediately, instead letting the quiet settle between them.

“You did good out there,” he said finally, his voice soft.

Ava let out a bitter laugh. “Good? I lost control, Gabriel. Again. I’m supposed to be helping, not scaring everyone.”

Gabriel leaned back, studying her expression. “You didn’t scare me.”

She turned to him, her eyes heavy with emotion. “You’re the only one, then.”

The vehicle hit a bump, jolting them slightly. Gabriel steadied himself before placing a hand on Ava’s shoulder. “Ava, they’re alive because of you. You stopped those mutants when no one else could. Don’t let their fear make you forget that.”

Ava sighed, her fingers tightening around the hilt of her blade. “I just… I don’t want to be this. I don’t want to be a monster.”

“You’re not a monster,” Gabriel said firmly. “You’re the reason we’re all still breathing.”

She glanced at him, searching his face for any sign of doubt. His gaze was steady, unwavering. For a moment, the tension in her chest eased.

The transport slowed as they approached a temporary checkpoint—a small outpost built into the remnants of an old warehouse. The team inside waved them through, their weapons at the ready. The vehicle pulled to a stop, and the survivors began to disembark, their movements slow and weary.

Gabriel and Ava stepped out last, the cool evening air a stark contrast to the stifling atmosphere inside the vehicle. The outpost was quiet but secure, its perimeter reinforced with steel barricades and makeshift turrets. It wasn’t much, but it was enough to give them a moment to breathe.

Harrow emerged from the vehicle, clutching the notebook tightly. His eyes darted around the outpost, taking in every detail. “What now?” he asked, his voice tinged with unease.

“We regroup,” Gabriel replied. “Jenna’s arranging for reinforcements. Until then, we wait.”

Ava wandered to the edge of the barricade, her gaze fixed on the horizon. The sun was setting, painting the sky in hues of orange and red. It was a rare moment of beauty in a world that seemed to offer so little of it.

Gabriel joined her, his presence quiet but grounding. For a long moment, neither of them spoke.

“Do you ever think about what it was like before?” Ava asked suddenly, her voice barely above a whisper.

Gabriel’s brow furrowed. “Sometimes. But it doesn’t help much.”

“I used to,” Ava said, her eyes softening as she glanced at the horizon. “Before all this, I was a botanist. I loved working with plants, understanding how they grew, how they thrived even in the harshest conditions. It felt like every new leaf or flower was a little miracle. I even remember the smell of the lab—soil, fresh-cut stems, and that faint, sweet scent of chlorophyll.” She paused, a wistful smile tugging at her lips. “It’s funny. That was over two hundred years ago, but some days, it feels like yesterday.”

Gabriel listened intently, his expression softening. “You’ve held onto that part of yourself,” he said gently. “The greenhouse project on the ship—everything you’re doing to keep life alive. That’s still you, Ava.”

She hesitated, her fingers brushing over the hilt of her blade. “I just worry… When I lose control, it feels like I’m slipping away. Like the person I used to be is fading, and all that’s left is—”

Gabriel interrupted her, stepping closer. “No. That person is still here. I see her every time you fight to protect us, every time you make sure we survive another day. You’re not just holding onto hope, Ava. You’re giving it to everyone around you.”

Her eyes searched his face, and for a moment, the sadness in them lifted. “Thanks, Gabriel,” she said softly, her voice laced with gratitude. “For seeing me.”

“Always,” he replied, his voice steady and filled with quiet affection.

The quiet stretched between them, not uncomfortable but layered with shared understanding. The world beyond the barricade was still dangerous, but for now, they had this moment of peace. It wasn’t much, but it was enough.

Ava’s lips twitched into a faint smile, though her eyes remained sad. She leaned closer, her voice soft and vulnerable. “Thanks, Gabriel. For always being here.”

Gabriel stepped closer, his hand brushing against hers before he wrapped his arms around her in a firm embrace. “I’m not going anywhere, Ava,” he murmured, his voice steady and warm.

She rested her head against his chest, listening to the steady rhythm of his heartbeat. For a moment, the chaos of the world seemed to fade. Ava tilted her face upward, meeting his gaze, and without hesitation, Gabriel leaned down, their lips meeting in a gentle but lingering kiss. The kiss was a quiet promise, filled with unspoken words they didn’t need to say.

When they finally pulled apart, Gabriel rested his forehead against hers, his voice barely above a whisper. “No matter what happens, we’ll face it together.”

Ava smiled, her sadness easing into something softer, more hopeful. “Together,” she echoed, her hand tightening around his as they stood there, letting the moment hold them.

The quiet stretched between them, not uncomfortable but heavy with unspoken thoughts. The world beyond the barricade was still dangerous, but for now, they had a moment of peace. It wasn’t much, but it was enough.
