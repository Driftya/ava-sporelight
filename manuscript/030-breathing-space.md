# Chapter 30: Breathing Space

The transport rattled hard enough to make speech difficult. Ava sat near the rear doors, bracing a boot against the opposite bench. Across from her, a survivor kept checking the latch after every jolt.

She had sheathed the cleaned knife but could still feel it in her hand. When she closed her eyes, she saw the factory pillar instead: the holes her spikes had left in metal.

Gabriel lowered himself onto the bench beside her, his rifle resting across his knees. He didn’t speak immediately, instead letting the quiet settle between them.

“You did good out there,” he said finally, his voice soft.

Ava let out a bitter laugh. “Good? I lost control, Gabriel. Again. I’m supposed to be helping, not scaring everyone.”

Gabriel leaned back, studying her expression. “You scared the shit out of me.”

She turned to him, her eyes heavy with emotion. “You didn’t say.”

The vehicle hit a bump. Gabriel caught the bench. “I was afraid you couldn’t hear me anymore. Then you told me to stay back.” He showed her the patch on his sleeve. “I should have given you more room even after you called me over.”

Ava sighed, her fingers tightening around the hilt of her blade. “I just… I don’t want to be this. I don’t want to be a monster.”

“Tell Malik exactly what happened. Including the part at the pillar.”

“You think he can stop it?”

“I think he needs to know it’s changing.”

Ava nodded. She would tell Malik about the pillar. She would also have to tell him how long she had needed to hold herself against it.

The transport slowed as they approached a temporary checkpoint—a small outpost built into the remnants of an old warehouse. The team inside waved them through, their weapons at the ready. The vehicle pulled to a stop, and the survivors began to disembark, their movements slow and weary.

Gabriel and Ava stepped out last. The outpost crew took them through decontamination and checked the air in the inner court before clearing them to remove their masks. Cool evening air reached Ava’s sweat-damp collar. Beyond the courtyard, guards stood behind reinforced steel barricades.

Harrow emerged from the vehicle, clutching the notebook tightly. His eyes darted around the outpost, taking in every detail. “What now?” he asked, his voice tinged with unease.

“We regroup,” Gabriel replied. “Jenna’s arranging for reinforcements. Until then, we wait.”

Inside, Ava watched the crew transfer the hospital folders and ledgers from her pack into two storage cases. She checked the contents before they sealed the lids. Gabriel handed over the scanner separately, identifying its incomplete copy of Harrow’s notebook for the records log.

Ava wandered to the edge of the barricade, her gaze fixed on the horizon. The sun was setting, painting the sky in hues of orange and red. It was a rare moment of beauty in a world that seemed to offer so little of it.

Gabriel joined her, his presence quiet but grounding. For a long moment, neither of them spoke.

“Do you ever think about what it was like before?” Ava asked suddenly, her voice barely above a whisper.

Gabriel’s brow furrowed. “Sometimes. But it doesn’t help much.”

“I remember leaving my field clothes on a chair,” Ava said. “Thinking I’d wash them the next day. There was soil on the sleeve.” She looked at the dressing around her hand. “The stupid things that stay.”

“Do you want to write it down?”

“When we get back.”

He nodded. “I’ll leave the journal out.”

She hesitated, her fingers brushing over the hilt of her blade. “I just worry… When I lose control, it feels like I’m slipping away. Like the person I used to be is fading, and all that’s left is—”

Gabriel waited until she finished trying to say it.

“I can’t tell you what it feels like,” he said. “But you corrected my grip while we were loading the vehicle.”

“What?”

“On the research pack. You told me I’d tear the pages.”

Ava remembered saying it, impatiently, while blood soaked through her dressing.

“I wanted it to get back intact,” she said.

“Then we should make sure it does.”

She stood beside him until the sun dropped below the warehouse roof.

Ava’s lips twitched into a faint smile, though her eyes remained sad. She leaned closer, her voice soft and vulnerable. “Thanks, Gabriel. For always being here.”

Gabriel stepped closer, his hand brushing against hers before he wrapped his arms around her in a firm embrace. “I’m not going anywhere, Ava,” he murmured, his voice steady and warm.

She rested her head against his chest, listening to the steady rhythm of his heartbeat. For a moment, the chaos of the world seemed to fade. Ava tilted her face upward, meeting his gaze, and without hesitation, Gabriel leaned down, their lips meeting in a gentle but lingering kiss. The kiss was a quiet promise, filled with unspoken words they didn’t need to say.

When they pulled apart, Gabriel brushed her cheek with his thumb. Ava caught his hand and kissed the knuckle where his glove had rubbed it raw.

Ava stayed close when the kiss ended. Gabriel’s coat fastener pressed into her cheek. She reached up to move it, then left her hand against his chest until someone called them in to eat.
