# Chapter 10: Breaking Barriers

Gabriel sat on the floor of the quarantine chamber, keeping his distance from Ava. She was still huddled in the far corner, her arms wrapped around her knees, her glowing amber eyes fixed on him. Her features were softer now, her breathing steadier, but she looked wary, like a cornered animal unsure whether to flee or fight.

He let the silence linger, giving her space. The sound of the ship’s hum filled the room, accompanied by the faint rattle of equipment in the observation area.

“You don’t have to talk,” Gabriel assured her, his words gentle yet firm as he broke the heavy silence between them. He sat a few feet away, his posture relaxed but attentive, making it clear he wasn’t a threat. “But I thought you might want to know where you are… what’s going on.” His gaze lingered on her, searching for any flicker of recognition or trust. The dim light cast shifting shadows on her face, highlighting the tension in her clenched jaw and the wary flicker in her amber eyes. She remained still, her breath uneven, as if balancing on the edge of fight or flight.

Ava didn’t respond, but she didn’t look away either. It was enough for Gabriel to continue.

“You’re on Haven’s Vanguard,” Gabriel explained, ushering her into the transport bay. “It’s a Titan-class Hover Carrier—our main operations base. We use it to coordinate large-scale rescues, deploy smaller extraction teams, and keep survivors safe.”

He paused, glancing at her as the hum of the ship’s fusion reactor reverberated faintly through the floor. “We’re heading north, to a checkpoint in the Arctic. It’s one of the last real safe zones we’ve got.”

Through the observation window, the sheer scale of the ship was evident. The colossal mobile fortress hovered over the frozen expanse below, supported by quad-turbofan engines that let it maneuver over the ruined world. Automated turrets lined the outer decks, standing guard against airborne threats, while reinforced titanium alloy plating bore scars of past battles.

Gabriel’s expression darkened slightly as he continued. “The world’s… different now. Changed. The spores spread faster in the heat. Cities fell. The land itself turned against us. That’s why we’re pushing north—cold slows the mutations down. It’s the only chance people have left.”

He studied Ava’s reaction, unsure of what he expected. Ava’s gaze shifted slightly, a flicker of curiosity in her eyes.

“A meteor struck Earth long before my parents were even born,” Gabriel continued, his expression darkening. “It didn’t just hit—it changed everything. It carried with it an unknown, alien life form, something we weren’t prepared for.” He exhaled sharply, the weight of history pressing down on him.

“The first signs were subtle—plants growing faster, animals behaving erratically—but then people started changing. Mutations spread slowly at first, contained in isolated outbreaks. But as temperatures rose, the spores embedded in the meteor's remains began to awaken. Global warming accelerated the spread, turning entire ecosystems into alien landscapes.”

Gabriel’s fingers tapped against his knee, his mind replaying the stories he had heard growing up. “Now, the only truly safe places left are where it’s cold enough to slow the mutations down. The spores don’t thrive in freezing temperatures. That’s why the rescue squads are focused on relocating survivors north, away from the infection zones.”

His gaze flicked toward Ava, watching for her reaction. “Most of us don’t fight these creatures directly,” he continued. “Killing them only makes it worse—when they die, they release spores, spreading the infection even further. That’s why we evacuate instead of engage.”

He let out a slow breath, shaking his head. “But some mutations are different. Some survive exposure in ways no one can explain.”

Her brow furrowed slightly, and Gabriel took it as a sign to keep going.

“Most of us don’t fight the life forms,” he continued. “Killing them releases spores, and that just makes things worse. Our job is to help people escape. Move them to safety.”

Ava’s fingers tightened around her knees. Her voice was barely above a whisper when she finally spoke. “Am I… one of them?”

Gabriel hesitated. He wasn’t sure how to answer. “You’re… different,” he said carefully. “But you’re not like them. Not completely.”

She looked away, her expression unreadable.

“I mean it,” Gabriel added, his voice firm. “Back in the broken city, when that thing attacked me… you saved me. You saved all of us. If it weren’t for you, we wouldn’t be here right now.”

Ava’s head turned slightly, her eyes meeting his. There was doubt in her gaze, but also a glimmer of something else—hope, maybe.

“I don’t remember much,” she admitted, her voice trembling. “I was sick. Dying. Then… I woke up here.”

Gabriel nodded. “I can’t imagine how scary that must be. But whatever happened to you, whatever they did… you’re still you. And you’re not alone in this.”

Ava studied him for a long moment, her guarded expression softening ever so slightly.

Over the next few days, their conversations grew longer. Gabriel kept his distance at first, sitting near the door while Ava stayed in her corner. He told her stories about his life before the mutations—his time as a firefighter, the small town he grew up in, and the parents he had lost to the life forms.

“They were good people,” he said one evening, his voice heavy with emotion. “They deserved better than what happened to them.”

Ava listened quietly, her head tilted slightly to the side. “You miss them.”

“Every day,” Gabriel admitted. He looked at her, his expression softening. “But I think… I think they’d be proud of what I’m doing now. Of helping people.”

Ava’s gaze dropped to the floor. “I don’t even know if I have parents.”

“You do,” Gabriel assured her, his voice steady yet thoughtful. “Everyone comes from somewhere… from someone. Even if you don’t remember them, they existed. And I’d bet that wherever they are—or were—they never stopped wanting you to survive.”

She didn’t respond, but the corners of her mouth twitched ever so slightly, as if she were considering a smile, or perhaps just the weight of his words.

By the time they were a few days away from the checkpoint, the distance between them had shrunk. Gabriel now sat a few feet away from Ava, close enough to see the faint shimmer of her skin in the dim light.

One evening, as they talked, Ava laughed softly at something he said. It was a quiet, almost shy sound, but it lit up her face in a way that took Gabriel by surprise.

“That’s the first time I’ve heard you laugh,” he remarked, his smile growing as he watched the warmth flicker in her eyes.

Ava hesitated, then gave him a small, tentative smile in return.

Gabriel felt his breath catch. Her features, even with their faint alien ridges, were strikingly beautiful. Her amber eyes glowed softly, framed by delicate strands of silver-threaded hair.

“You’re amazing,” he said before he could stop himself.

Ava’s smile faded slightly, and she looked away. “I’m not. I’m… a freak.”

“No,” Gabriel said firmly. He reached out instinctively, stopping himself just before his fingers brushed against her hand. He hesitated, watching her carefully—the way she avoided his gaze, the tension in her shoulders, the doubt shadowing her expression.

She had no idea what she was. No idea what she had done—what she could do. She had saved him without even trying, without understanding how or why. That kind of power was terrifying, unpredictable. But it was also something else. Something rare.

He swallowed, his thoughts tangling between caution and awe. If she could destroy those creatures by accident, what would happen if she learned to control it? To wield it? The idea unsettled him, but not because he feared her. No—what unnerved him was the realization that, for the first time in a long while, he felt something dangerously close to hope.

“You’re strong. You’re brave,” he said at last, his voice quieter now, more certain. “And after what you did for us back there… I think you might be humanity’s last hope.”

She looked at him, her eyes wide, searching his face for any sign of doubt. “You don’t mean that.”

“I do,” Gabriel said. “You’re the only one I’ve ever seen who can stop them without making things worse. That’s not something to be afraid of, Ava. That’s something to hold onto.”

For a moment, neither of them spoke. Then Ava’s lips curved into another small smile, her eyes glistening with unshed tears.

Gabriel’s chest tightened as he watched her, a mixture of admiration and fear swirling inside him. He couldn’t deny how much he cared for her already, but the thought of what the world might do to someone like her… it terrified him.

“They’ll never understand you,” he said quietly, almost to himself.

Ava tilted her head. “Who?”

“People,” Gabriel said, his voice heavy. “If they find out what you can do, what you are… they’ll be afraid. And when people are afraid, they do terrible things.”

Ava’s smile faded, and she hugged her knees to her chest. “I don’t want to hurt anyone.”

“I know,” Gabriel said. He moved closer, sitting beside her. “And I’ll do everything I can to make sure no one hurts you either.”

For the first time since they met, Ava leaned against him, her head resting lightly on his shoulder. Gabriel stayed still, his heart pounding, but he didn’t pull away.

Whatever lay ahead, he knew one thing for certain: Ava was worth protecting.
