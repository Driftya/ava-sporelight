# Chapter 21: Comfort in Shadows

The faint light of dawn crept over the jagged skyline, turning the edges of broken buildings into glimmers of hope. The previous night had been tense but productive. Gabriel had managed to contact Jenna through the long-distance radio, a tenuous signal crackling with interference. He relayed the situation about the survivors, requesting a team to extract them and transfer them to safety. Jenna’s response was curt but reassuring: help would come, though it might take a day or two to arrive.

Ava stirred awake, the warmth of Gabriel’s jacket still draped over her shoulders. The fire had burned low, its embers casting a soft glow across the room. Harrow was curled up in the corner, snoring faintly, while the survivors huddled together on the far side of the barricaded space.

She shifted slightly, the faint crackle of the dying fire filling the quiet. The room was still, save for the slow, steady breaths of those around her—some deep and rhythmic, others shallow and uneasy. The weight of exhaustion clung to them all, their bodies pressed close for warmth, their slumber fragile, always on the edge of wakefulness. In the silence, Ava could almost hear the unspoken fears lingering in the air, the restless dreams of those who had lost everything, searching for solace in sleep they would never truly find.

Gabriel sat by the entrance, his rifle resting across his lap. His eyes were fixed on the horizon, scanning for any sign of movement. He didn’t look away as Ava approached, her footsteps soft against the cracked concrete.

“You didn’t sleep,” she said, her voice hushed.

Gabriel shook his head. “Someone had to keep watch. Besides, you needed it more than I did.”

Ava sat beside him, her legs crossed beneath her. She glanced at him, noticing the tension in his shoulders and the faint shadows under his eyes. “You’re allowed to take care of yourself too, you know.”

He let out a quiet laugh, the sound dry but genuine. “I’ll sleep when we get to the hospital. Until then, someone has to make sure we get there in one piece.”

For a moment, neither of them spoke. The stillness of the early morning wrapped around them like a fragile cocoon, sheltering them from the chaos waiting beyond the barricades.

“Do you think they’ll trust me?” Ava asked suddenly, her gaze fixed on the distant horizon.

Gabriel turned to look at her, his expression softening. “They’re scared. But they’ll come around. People always do once they see what you’re capable of.”

“That’s what I’m afraid of,” she admitted. “What if they see too much? What if it’s not enough?”

Gabriel reached out, his hand resting lightly on her arm. “Ava, you’ve saved more lives than most of them could dream of. You’re not just enough—you’re everything they need right now.”

Her throat tightened at his words, but she nodded, letting them sink in. She leaned against him slightly, drawing comfort from his steady presence. They stayed that way for a while, watching as the sun climbed higher, casting light over the desolation.

The sound of movement behind them broke the moment. Harrow shuffled over, rubbing his eyes. “Morning,” he muttered, his voice groggy.

Gabriel rose to his feet, his rifle back in hand. "Jenna’s team won’t arrive until tomorrow. We’ll secure this camp and make sure the survivors stay safe here. Then we push toward the hospital."

Harrow hesitated, his expression torn. “I’m coming with you,” he said finally. “There’s something I need to find there.”

Gabriel raised an eyebrow but didn’t question him. “Alright. Just stay close and don’t wander off. We can’t afford to lose anyone.”

Ava glanced at Harrow, curiosity flickering in her eyes, but she didn’t press him. Whatever his reasons, she knew better than to pry now. The survivors were beginning to stir, their faces still etched with fear but carrying a flicker of hope. She caught Gabriel’s eye, his silent nod reassuring her. Together, they would lead them forward, one step at a time.”
