# Chapter 17: The Price of Survival

The world outside was as unforgiving as ever. The crumbling remnants of civilization stretched endlessly, with skeletal buildings looming like gravestones, their once-bustling interiors now silent tombs. Thick layers of dust clung to every surface, and the occasional sound of distant howls reminded everyone that they were not alone.

The ship had landed near what was once a suburban neighborhood, now overtaken by twisted vegetation and eerie stillness. Gabriel led the scavenging group, moving cautiously through the streets with his gas mask secured. Ava was back on the ship, tending to the greenhouse, though her mind was far from the plants.

She couldn’t shake the unease that had settled in her chest since Gabriel left. She had grown used to his presence, his voice, his warmth. Now, the silence felt oppressive.

The scavenging mission was going smoothly at first. Gabriel and his team found a small grocery store that hadn’t been completely ransacked. While most of the canned goods were spoiled or rusted, they managed to gather enough supplies to make the trip worthwhile.

But as they prepared to leave, a sound stopped them in their tracks—a guttural clicking noise that sent shivers down their spines.

“Move! Back to the vehicle!” Gabriel ordered, his voice muffled through the mask.

The team scrambled, but the clicking grew louder, and a mutated life form burst from the shadows. Its elongated limbs and sharp, crystalline growths glinted in the dim light. It moved with terrifying speed, lunging toward one of the survivors.

Gabriel fired his weapon, aiming for distraction rather than a kill to avoid spore release. The creature snarled and turned its attention to him. Gabriel backed away, drawing it further from the group.

In the chaos, a survivor tripped, and the creature lunged. Gabriel didn’t hesitate—he threw himself between the monster and the fallen man, taking a sharp blow to his side as he shoved the other to safety.

The team managed to escape, but at a cost. Two survivors had been lost, and Gabriel was bleeding heavily from a deep gash on his torso.

Back on the ship, Ava’s heart stopped when she saw him stagger through the airlock, his arm slung over another survivor’s shoulder. Blood stained his shirt, and his steps were unsteady.

“Gabriel!” she cried, rushing to his side.

“I’m fine,” he said weakly, though the pain in his voice betrayed him.

“Don’t lie to me,” she whispered, helping him to the medical bay.

As Jenna worked to patch him up, Ava hovered anxiously. When Jenna reassured her that there was no infection, Ava felt a surge of relief—but it was quickly replaced by a fierce determination.

She couldn’t stand by any longer.

That evening, as Gabriel rested, Ava approached Jenna. “I want to help with the scavenging missions.”

Jenna frowned. “Ava, you’re already doing so much here. You don’t have to—”

“I want to,” Ava interrupted, her voice firm. “I can’t just stay here and wait for him to come back hurt—or worse. I can handle the life forms better than anyone. I’m immune to their spores, and you know I can fight.”

Jenna studied her for a long moment before nodding. “I’ll talk to Gabriel. But you know he’s going to argue.”

“He can argue all he wants,” Ava said quietly. “I’m not changing my mind.”

Gabriel wasn’t happy when he found out, but after hours of heated discussions, he reluctantly agreed. “If you’re going out there, I’m going with you,” he insisted.

Training and First Missions

Before she could join the next operation, Ava needed to be prepared. Gabriel made sure of that.

For the next several days, he drilled her relentlessly, starting with the basics—how to handle a weapon, where to strike to incapacitate rather than kill, how to anticipate enemy movements. Though Ava had instinctive strength and speed, combat was more than raw power. It was about control, about knowing when to hold back and when to strike.

“Don’t just rely on your strength,” Gabriel told her, stepping back after demonstrating a proper defensive stance. “It’s an advantage, sure, but without control, it’s just another liability.”

Ava wiped the sweat from her brow and adjusted her stance, her fingers curling into fists. She wasn’t used to fighting this way—structured, calculated. She had always reacted, letting instinct take over, but now she had to think. Gabriel made her repeat each move until it felt natural, until her footwork was precise and her strikes landed with intent rather than blind aggression.

The training wasn’t just physical. Gabriel also taught her about the dangers of the field—how mutations behaved, the best ways to avoid unnecessary fights, and the importance of staying calm under pressure.

“If you panic, you make mistakes,” he said, tossing her a knife. She barely caught it in time, fingers fumbling around the hilt. “And out there? One mistake can get you—or someone else—killed.”

Ava swallowed, gripping the knife tighter. She understood that better than anyone.

Her first mission didn’t go as planned.

The team was sent on a small supply run to a derelict convenience store on the outskirts of a ruined city. It was supposed to be an easy job—go in, grab what they could carry, and get out before attracting attention.

But when a group of twisted, skeletal mutations emerged from the shadows, Ava hesitated. The way they moved, their gnarled limbs twitching unnaturally, sent ice through her veins. The others reacted immediately—Gabriel, leading the charge, took out two with quick, calculated shots. The rest of the team moved in sync, covering each other.

But Ava?

She stood frozen, breath hitching as memories clawed at the edges of her mind. The lab. The restraints. The sterile voices discussing her like she was an object. The burning pain of her mutation overtaking her body.

One of the creatures lunged at her. She barely moved in time, stumbling backward as it swiped at her, claws grazing her arm. Her blood sizzled where it touched, the creature shrieking as it recoiled. The noise jolted her back to reality.

“Ava! Move!” Gabriel’s voice cut through her haze.

She tried. She swung at the creature, but her movements were sloppy, panicked. It wasn’t like training—it was chaotic, messy, real.

She almost lost control.

The fear surged too quickly, her heart hammering as her body tensed, the glow in her veins intensifying. Her mutation threatened to take over, to consume the fear with violence. She could feel it creeping up, the familiar sensation of something monstrous clawing beneath her skin.

Then—Gabriel was there.

His hands gripped her shoulders, firm but steady, grounding her. “Ava, breathe,” he ordered. His voice was low, calm—not angry, not afraid. Just solid.

Her body trembled, the energy inside her teetering on the edge, but she focused on his voice, on his presence. Slowly, the glow receded.

Gabriel let out a breath and squeezed her shoulder before turning back to the fight.

They survived that mission, but Ava couldn’t forget how close she had come to losing herself.

Still, she refused to quit.

Gabriel didn’t give up on her either. After each mission, he reviewed what went wrong and how to improve. She worked harder, learning to anticipate her fear instead of running from it. She learned how to fight with control, how to channel her power without letting it consume her.

And then, finally, the time came for her to prove herself.

The Factory Settlement Mission

They set out with a smaller team, this time heading to a makeshift settlement built within the ruins of an old factory. The survivors there had done their best to fortify the area with scrap metal and barbed wire, but their resources were dwindling, and their defenses weren’t enough to keep the mutations at bay.

Ava had been on missions before, but this was different. This time, she felt ready.

The settlement had an air of desperation. People clung to what little they had, their faces gaunt and their eyes wary. Children played in the dirt, their laughter a rare sound in a world so bleak.

Ava felt a pang in her chest as she looked around. These people needed help, and she couldn’t ignore their plight.

While negotiating with the settlement’s leader, a mutation attacked. The creature was unlike any Ava had faced before—a hulking beast with multiple limbs and a maw filled with razor-sharp teeth. It tore through the settlement’s defenses, scattering the residents.

Ava froze for only a second—then she saw Gabriel among the chaos, trying to help the others. Fear threatened to take hold, but this time, she pushed past it.

Her fists clenched, glowing faintly as her mutation flared—not from fear, but from determination.

She charged at the creature, dodging its massive claws. The fight was brutal—every punch and kick she landed felt like hitting solid steel, and the creature’s strikes left her bruised and battered. But she refused to back down.

It lunged, knocking her to the ground, its maw snapping inches from her face. Her heart pounded, and for a terrifying moment, she felt that old fear creeping in.

Then—Gabriel’s voice.

“Ava! You’ve got this!”

His belief in her was like a rope pulling her back from the abyss.

With one last, desperate blow, she drove her hand into the creature’s chest, her toxic blood searing through its flesh. The beast let out a final, guttural roar before collapsing.

Ava stumbled back, breathing hard. The settlement was silent, everyone staring at her. Some with awe, others with fear.

Gabriel approached, his gaze sweeping over her with quiet intensity, not just assessing her injuries but feeling the weight of what she had just done. His chest ached at the sight of her—her hands still clenched into trembling fists, blood dripping from between her fingers where she had torn her own skin to unleash the toxic force within her. Her breath came in sharp, uneven gulps, her body swaying slightly from exhaustion.

“Ava…” His voice was gentle, almost hesitant, as if he wasn’t sure whether to reach for her or give her space. “Are you okay?”

She swallowed, flexing her fingers as the glow in her veins dimmed, leaving only the raw sting of open wounds in its wake. A sharp pulse of pain radiated through her hands, her knuckles slick with her own blood, the cost of what she had done. She let out a shaky breath, her body screaming at her, but she forced herself to nod.

“Yeah,” she said, though her voice wavered. “I am.”

Gabriel exhaled, but there was no relief in his expression—only something heavier, something that settled deep behind his eyes. He had watched her fight, had cheered for her, but now, looking at her like this, he felt something else entirely. Guilt.

She was strong—stronger than anyone he had ever met—but that strength came at a price. A price she kept paying in blood.

His fingers twitched at his side before he finally moved, reaching out with quiet hesitation. He brushed a strand of damp hair from her face, his touch featherlight, as if afraid she might shatter beneath it.

“You don’t have to do this alone,” he murmured. His voice was steady, but there was something raw in it—something that made Ava’s chest tighten.

She held his gaze, her breath still uneven, but for an entirely different reason now. The exhaustion in his eyes, the way his jaw tensed as he looked at her hands—it wasn’t just worry. It was something deeper. He hated seeing her like this.

“I know,” she whispered, the words barely more than a breath. But as she said them, she wasn’t sure if she was reassuring him or herself.

For the first time, she wasn’t just afraid of losing control.

She was afraid of what it meant that someone cared this much if she did.

By the time they returned to the ship, they had recruited a few survivors from the settlement—people willing to work and help rebuild. The atmosphere on the ship was tense but hopeful.

That night, as Ava sat in the greenhouse, Gabriel joined her. They didn’t speak for a long time, simply enjoying the quiet.

The gentle hum of the ship vibrated through the metal floor, a steady undercurrent to the rhythmic dripping of water from the irrigation system. The air smelled of damp soil and fresh greenery, a stark contrast to the cold steel of the corridors beyond. Ava sat on the ground, her legs tucked beneath her, absently brushing dirt from her fingertips as she stared at the delicate seedlings pushing through the soil—fragile, yet determined.

Gabriel leaned against the doorway, arms crossed, watching her in silence. His gaze wasn’t searching for wounds or assessing damage like it usually did after a mission. It was softer, more thoughtful.

When he finally spoke, his voice was quiet but firm. “You were brave today.”

Ava’s fingers stilled against the dirt. Her first instinct was to brush it off, to say it wasn’t bravery, just survival. But she hesitated.

Gabriel stepped closer, his boots barely making a sound against the metal floor. “And you endured,” he added. “Even when it hurt. Even when it should’ve been too much.”

She swallowed, feeling the weight of those words settle deep in her chest. Brave. Endured. They weren’t the same. One was about pushing forward despite fear. The other was about bearing it.

Ava let out a slow breath, flexing her hands, wincing slightly as the cuts across her knuckles pulled tight. “It didn’t feel brave,” she admitted, her voice quieter now. “It felt like I was drowning, like I had to fight just to stay above the surface.”

Gabriel’s jaw tightened. “That’s what makes it brave.”

Ava looked up at him then, something in his tone pulling her gaze to his. The way he said it—not as praise, not as reassurance, but as fact—made her chest tighten. He meant it.

The silence between them stretched, heavy with everything unsaid.

She turned back to the plants, running her fingers gently over the tiny leaves, grounding herself in the sensation. “They’re going to need more space soon,” she murmured, shifting the subject before her emotions could get the better of her. “Stronger roots.”

Gabriel studied her for a long moment, then nodded. “Yeah,” he said, his voice softer now. “They will.”

And the way he looked at her made her chest tighten. She could feel her emotions bubbling to the surface—fear, admiration, and something else she wasn’t ready to name.

Gabriel reached out, his fingers brushing against hers. It was a small gesture, but it sent a shiver down her spine.

“Get some rest,” he said softly, standing to leave.

As he walked away, Ava pressed a hand to her chest, feeling the rapid beat of her heart. She didn’t know what the future held, but for the first time in a long while, she allowed herself to hope.
