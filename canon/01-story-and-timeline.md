## Part I — The Last Experiment

### Chapters 1–2: botanist and infection

Ava begins as a young botanist in a living world. The meteor-borne organism is initially a scientific discovery, not a known apocalypse. Exposure makes her sick. Instead of receiving care, she is taken by people who see her unusual survival as research material.

This part must preserve Ava’s identity before mutation. She is not created in the laboratory. She is a human being infected first and violated by experiments afterward.

### Chapters 3–5: captivity and transformation

The researchers expose Ava to concentrated variants and invasive procedures. Her body integrates what kills or degrades other subjects. The result gives her extraordinary resilience while beginning a progressive mutation she cannot safely control.

When containment fails, Ava’s stress drives a violent monstrous transformation. A scientist secures her in cryogenic suspension as the facility collapses around them.

### Chapter 6: the two-century bridge

The facility’s breach accelerates the wider ecological catastrophe. Plants, animals, and humans change. Civilization fragments. Cold regions become humanity’s strongest refuges. Rescue squads emerge during the centuries Ava remains frozen.

## Part II — The Rescue

### Chapters 7–10: discovery and quarantine

Roughly two hundred years after the original outbreak, Gabriel finds Ava’s functioning cryopod during an evacuation. Her awakening is disoriented and violent. Her fresh blood or direct biological contact kills mutant life forms without producing the same spore-release danger as ordinary weapons.

Gabriel sees both her danger and her personhood. Jenna and the rescue organization initially emphasize quarantine, examination, and protocol. Ava’s fear of being used again makes clinical treatment especially destabilizing.

### Chapters 11–14: trust, fortress, and escape

Ava and Gabriel slowly establish trust. Her fragmented history and the crew’s fear remain active pressures. At the fortress, fear and containment reproduce elements of her original captivity. A new disaster pushes Ava toward loss of control.

Gabriel refuses to abandon her. They survive the fortress collapse, cross the frozen terrain, and return to Haven’s Vanguard. The surviving crew remain divided about allowing Ava aboard.

## Part III — A Fragile Sanctuary

### Chapters 15–16: belonging takes root

The carrier is safety without immediate acceptance. Cora, Malik, Rhea, Marcus, Cole, Jenna, and other crew members embody different responses to Ava: practicality, care, loyalty, curiosity, or fear.

Ava converts an unused cargo area into a greenhouse. The work reconnects her to botany, feeds the crew’s hope, and gives her a contribution that is neither violence nor biological research. Gabriel gives her a journal before she begins using it. His absence on missions helps both characters recognize how much they rely on one another.

Their attraction remains unspoken. Gabriel eventually shares the burden of losing his parents; Ava grieves the family who lived and died during her suspension. Their intimacy grows through mutual vulnerability rather than rescue debt.

### Chapters 17–18: fighting and love

Gabriel returns injured from a mission. Ava refuses to remain a protected bystander and asks to join field operations. Training teaches her structure, restraint, and recovery rather than merely increasing her power.

The factory settlement mission proves that she can act through fear without surrendering completely to mutation. After the mission arc, Ava and Gabriel explicitly confess their love. Their kiss and sexual intimacy occur here, before the hospital mission.

## Part IV — Scavengers in the Shadows

### Chapters 19–25: the hospital mission

Ava is already an active fighter, the greenhouse already exists, and her romantic relationship with Gabriel has begun. The team enters ruined territory to recover medical research from a hospital. The mission introduces Harrow as a useful but unreliable scavenger.

### Chapters 26–32: pressure, transformation, and betrayal

Mutant attacks and environmental danger push Ava toward a stronger monstrous state. Gabriel remains her tactical and emotional anchor, but he cannot control the mutation for her.

Harrow betrays the group and escapes with part of the research. Ava and Gabriel preserve the remaining notes and return to the carrier with survivors. The mission ends in loss, but not failure.

## Part V — A New Dawn

### Chapter 33: consent and research

Jenna catalogs the surviving hospital notes and cross-references their blood work with Ava’s medical data. Dr. Elias Karrow proposes controlled study of Ava’s blood as a possible basis for treatment.

The ethical center is consent. Ava has been used as a specimen before. This time she chooses whether to participate, establishes conditions, and trusts Gabriel to perform the blood draw under Jenna’s guidance.

### Chapters 34–35: the future

Two years later, a vaccine derived from Ava’s blood is effective in limited production. Humanity is not restored overnight, but communities have a credible path away from extinction.

Ava is pregnant with Gabriel’s child. The ending completes her movement from stolen body and stolen future to chosen love, chosen scientific participation, and a family she can help protect.

## Required Ordering Rules

- The origin chapters must precede Gabriel’s discovery.
- The fortress escape must precede the sanctuary and greenhouse scenes.
- Gabriel’s injury must precede Ava’s structured combat training.
- The factory settlement must precede the love confession.
- The confession and first intimate night must precede the romantic behavior in the hospital arc.
- Harrow’s theft and the recovered hospital notes must precede Karrow’s proposal.
- Harrow and Karrow are different people.
- The two-year jump begins only after Ava consents to the blood research.
