## Narrative Red Line

Every scene should serve at least one stage of the established progression:

**origin → rescue → belonging → fighting together → love → betrayal and discovery → chosen sacrifice → future**

Action should expose character. Romance should change decisions. Worldbuilding should create pressure or possibility rather than interrupt the story to explain itself.

## Point of View and Tone

- Primary mode: close third person, most often through Ava or Gabriel.
- Genre balance: post-apocalyptic science fiction, body horror, action, and romance.
- Emotional register: intimate and vulnerable without making danger decorative.
- Violence: physical, consequential, and sometimes brutal.
- Mutation: both power and bodily violation; never a clean superhero upgrade.
- Romance: a source of agency and risk, not a cure for trauma or mutation.
- Hope: material and earned—food grown, people rescued, consent respected, research recovered—not merely declared.

Do not soften the manuscript’s violence, sexual intimacy, fear, or emotional intensity. Revision may sharpen language, remove repetition, or improve consent and continuity, but it must not euphemize the intended content.

## Terminology

- **Spores:** the microscopic alien biological agents.
- **Mutant life form / mutation:** preferred general terms for changed organisms.
- **Infected person:** a person in the progressive process before or during loss of identity.
- **Ava’s transformed form:** Ava under acute or advanced mutation pressure; do not call this a separate personality unless canon later establishes one.
- **Haven’s Vanguard / the carrier / the ship:** the Titan-class hover carrier. “Ship” does not mean spacecraft.
- **Fortress:** the frozen refuge destroyed during chapters 13–14; no proper name is established.
- **Harrow:** the scavenger and betrayer.
- **Dr. Elias Karrow:** the later scientist. Never shorten his name in a way that makes him easy to confuse with Harrow.

## Character Guardrails

### Ava

- She is dangerous and deserves autonomy; neither truth cancels the other.
- She was not built from nothing. Her human life as a botanist matters.
- She survives mutation but continues changing toward a potentially permanent monster state.
- She does not need Gabriel’s permission to act, though his concern can create real conflict.
- Self-injury for toxic blood must carry physical and emotional cost.

### Gabriel

- Protective behavior must not become unquestioned ownership.
- His rescue instinct can be noble, reckless, or both.
- He grounds Ava through trust and familiarity, not supernatural control.
- He has goals and grief beyond romance.

### Jenna

- She is both commander and scientist.
- Protocol is a value and a flaw, not shorthand for cruelty.
- Her changed relationship with Ava requires remembered accountability.

### Harrow and Karrow

- Harrow represents fearful self-preservation and betrayal.
- Karrow represents the ethical danger and possible value of science.
- They are unrelated unless future canon explicitly says otherwise.

## Continuity Checklist

Before accepting a revision, check:

- Does the chapter number and stable ID remain correct?
- Does the scene occur before or after the fortress, greenhouse, training, confession, hospital betrayal, or two-year jump?
- Has Ava already learned the skill she uses?
- Is Ava’s blood fresh enough for the claimed combat effect?
- Does a mutant death risk spore release, and is protection addressed?
- Is Gabriel wearing protection in contaminated environments?
- Is Haven’s Vanguard behaving as a hover carrier rather than a spacecraft?
- Does Ava retain the final choice over research or medical contact?
- Are Harrow and Karrow clearly distinguished?
- Has repeated reassurance, attraction, or greenhouse description been removed rather than stacked?
- Does new material contradict the final family outcome?

## Material Outside This Novel

*Sporelight Gabriel*, *Daughter of the New Dawn*, Aeris, Kael, Nyra, faction campaigns, and game mission chains are reserved series material. They may draw on this canon, but they do not retroactively establish facts in *Ava: Sporelight*.

The phrase blacklist and inspiration notes are editorial aids, not story facts. Use them to challenge clichés, but do not let mechanical phrase avoidance erase the novel’s recurring images of spores, roots, light, cold, and growth.
