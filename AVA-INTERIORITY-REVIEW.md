# Ava’s Inner Life — Revision Review, 2026-09-13

The first revision expanded all 35 chapters while retaining the publishing copyedit’s structure, chronology, rescue logistics, research custody, and mature content. Approximately 8,100 words were added, increasing the numbered manuscript from about 30,800 to 39,000 words, or roughly 26%. These approximate counts include chapter headings and metadata. Expansion was concentrated where an experience needed time to register; chapters were not mechanically doubled.

## Second pass — immediate voice and raw feeling

The author found that the expanded prose still described Ava’s emotions more often than it let the reader experience their unprocessed language. The second pass retains the first revision and adds about 1,000 words, bringing the 35 numbered chapters to approximately 40,000 words. Brief first-person direct thoughts now interrupt the close-third narration at pressure points, while cries, unfinished sentences, anger, pleading, and less composed dialogue carry the peaks themselves.

The main changes are deliberately uneven. Captivity and awakening use short survival thoughts and repeated refusals; grief is allowed to become a sob or an impossible appeal to an absent parent; crew rejection produces impulses to flee, hide, or demand an answer; combat includes the frightening wish to surrender to the mutation as well as the shame afterward. Chapter 18 renders Ava’s attraction and orgasm through her own bodily attention without changing consent or turning intimacy into a clinical checklist. Chapter 31 now lets Harrow’s injury to Gabriel provoke immediate disbelief, screaming rage, a wish to kill Harrow, panic over Ava’s changing hands, and pleading before Ava can recover enough to choose care over pursuit. Labour and early motherhood likewise include pain, anger, touch aversion, guilt, and love at the same time.

The temporary origin, sanctuary, main-story, and hospital drafts were revisited for their self-talk, longing, vulnerability, erotic openness, and emotional volume. Their generalized summaries, duplicate reassurance, continuity errors, omniscient leakage, and noncanonical claims remain excluded. No additional game location or faction was promoted in this pass; the earlier limited worldbuilding promotions remain sufficient for the present novel.

## What was missing

The copyedit preserved important physical and emotional details, especially in the greenhouse, confession, and research-consent scenes. The gap was sustained access to what Ava privately makes of an experience. Readers could often see her fear or hear her reassurance without spending enough time with her conflicting wishes, unspoken embarrassment, ordinary pleasures, and grief after the immediate danger passed.

The revision gives her more than suffering to reveal. Before infection she enjoys her work, imagines recognition, and tires of chosen solitude. Later she can resent help and need it, want strangers to accept her and be angry at them, feel desire while remaining shy, or save someone without feeling triumphant. Those contradictions belong to Ava as an individual; they are not presented as universal female emotions.

Her lost family and former life recur with different meanings: disbelief after waking, the failure to recover faces, the comfort of remembering an ordinary impatience, and the wish to show her parents their granddaughter. Gabriel’s love does not settle those losses. Ava also has feelings and choices in scenes with Jenna, Cora, Malik, civilians, and alone with her journal.

## Worldbuilding and canon

The author explicitly invited selective use of game locations and factions. The regional descriptions and faction profiles in the temporary datasets were checked against the novel’s geography, society, and chronology. Four names receive limited promotion: the Frozen North, the Ruined Heartland, the Northern Coalition, and the Crimson Syndicate. Their scope is documented in [World, Locations, and Society](canon/04-world-locations-and-society.md).

These additions support existing travel and social pressure. They establish no faction command over the carrier or fortress, no identity for Harrow’s buyer, no new journey, and no universal currency. Other faction campaigns, geographical assignments, sequel material, and game mechanics remain reserved. Cold continues to suppress rather than eliminate spore danger.

The durable emotional brief, selected promotions, and mutation-reporting clarification are recorded in the [continuity ledger](canon/07-decisions-and-continuity-ledger.md). The [prose guide](canon/08-prose-style-guide.md) now explicitly protects interior thought alongside bodily detail, including Ava’s own experience of attraction.
